# **2\. Una decisión no termina cuando se elige**

Elegir:

A \> B \> C

no completa el trabajo.

Debemos ser capaces de explicar:

WHY A?

WHY NOW?

WHY BEFORE B?

WHAT EVIDENCE?

WHAT ASSUMPTIONS?

WHAT UNCERTAINTY?

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE DECISION?

El producto final de F4 no es:

SCORE \= 91.7

Es una:

DECISION  
\+  
RATIONALE  
\+  
EVIDENCE  
\+  
UNCERTAINTY  
\+  
CONDITIONS

# **4\. Explainability**

Conceptualmente:

DECISION EXPLAINABILITY

es la capacidad de comunicar de forma comprensible:

> **Por qué una determinada opción recibió precedencia sobre las alternativas relevantes.**

---

# **5\. Explainability ≠ Verbosity**

Una explicación de:

10,000 words

no es necesariamente mejor que una de:

10 lines

si estas contienen los factores verdaderamente decisivos.

---

# **6\. Explanation Should Reveal Decision Drivers**

La pregunta es:

WHAT ACTUALLY  
DROVE THE DECISION?

---

# **7\. Decisive Factors**

Una buena explicación identifica:

THE FEW FACTORS  
THAT MOST CHANGED  
THE RANKING.

---

# **8\. Not Every Input Is Equally Important**

Podemos haber analizado:

20 dimensions

pero quizá la decisión gira principalmente alrededor de:

CURRENT CONSTRAINT

COST OF DELAY

CASH RISK

---

# **9\. Explain Drivers, Not Database Dumps**

Incorrecto:

Impact high.  
Urgency high.  
Fit medium-high.  
Effort medium.  
Risk medium.  
Confidence 0.78.

Eso describe.

No necesariamente explica.

# **10\. Better Explanation**

Inventory ranks above CRO because  
profitable demand is already being  
lost through stock limitation.

Additional CRO would increase demand  
that current supply cannot fulfill,  
while supplier lead time means  
waiting increases the probability  
of a stockout.

# **11\. Why This?**

Primera pregunta obligatoria:

WHY THIS PRIORITY?

Debe conectar el candidato con:

BUSINESS OUTCOME.

---

# **12\. Why Now?**

Segunda:

WHY NOW?

Debe explicar:

TIME

WINDOW

DAMAGE VELOCITY

DEPENDENCY

CURRENT STATE

cuando sean relevantes.

---

# **13\. Why Before the Others?**

Tercera:

WHY BEFORE B / C / D?

Esta es una de las preguntas más importantes de F4.

---

# **14\. Absolute Importance Is Not Enough**

Decir:

A is important.

no explica por qué:

A \> B.

---

# **15\. Relative Rationale**

Debe explicar el trade-off:

A has slightly lower total upside than B,  
but B can tolerate a two-month delay  
while A's intervention window closes  
within two weeks.

---

# **16\. What Happens If We Wait?**

Cuarta pregunta:

WHAT HAPPENS  
IF WE DO NOT ACT NOW?

---

# **17\. Cost of Delay Explanation**

Puede ser:

loss continues

option expires

risk compounds

constraint worsens

nothing material happens

---

# **18\. “Nothing Material Happens” Is Valid**

No toda prioridad necesita dramatización.

---

# **19\. What Would Change the Decision?**

Quinta pregunta esencial:

WHAT WOULD MAKE  
US CHANGE OUR MIND?

---

# **20\. Falsifiability**

Una decisión es más útil cuando puede especificar:

CONDITIONS UNDER WHICH  
IT WOULD NO LONGER  
BE PREFERRED.

---

# **21\. Rank-Switch Condition**

Ejemplo:

Inventory remains \#1 unless  
the supplier confirms replenishment  
before stock coverage reaches  
the critical threshold.

---

# **22\. Unfalsifiable Recommendation**

Mala:

Retention is important  
because retention is always important.

No existe condición observable que pueda cuestionarla.

---

# **23\. Conditional Decision**

Mejor:

Retention becomes the leading structural  
priority once inventory headroom is restored,  
provided repeat economics remain attractive.

---

# **24\. Trazabilidad**

Conceptualmente:

DECISION TRACEABILITY

es la capacidad de reconstruir:

> **Cómo se llegó desde la evidencia disponible hasta la decisión final.**

# **26\. Why Traceability Matters**

Sin ella:

DECISION

OPINION WITH AUTHORITY.

# **27\. Traceability Does Not Mean Exposing Hidden Reasoning**

EVIDENCE

# **28\. Evidence Trace**

Cada afirmación decisiva debería poder conectarse a:

WHERE DO WE KNOW THIS FROM?

---

# **29\. Evidence Types**

Pueden incluir:

FIRST-PARTY DATA

FINANCIAL DATA

OPERATIONAL DATA

CUSTOMER DATA

EXPERIMENTS

DOCUMENTED BUSINESS CONTEXT

EXTERNAL EVIDENCE

EXPERT ASSUMPTION

---

# **30\. Evidence Strength Differs**

No debemos tratar:

verified transaction data

igual que:

founder impression.

---

# **31\. But Qualitative Evidence Can Matter**

Ejemplo:

supplier explicitly states  
production capacity is exhausted.

Puede ser decisivo aunque no aparezca en analytics.

---

# **32\. Evidence Provenance**

Pregunta:

WHERE DID THIS  
DECISION-RELEVANT FACT  
COME FROM?

---

# **33\. Freshness**

Otra:

HOW OLD IS IT?

---

# **34\. Stale Evidence**

Un supuesto válido hace seis meses puede no describir el ecommerce actual.

---

# **35\. Evidence Relevance**

No toda evidencia verdadera es relevante para la decisión.

---

# **36\. Example**

CTR improved 15%.

Puede ser cierto.

Pero si la decisión es:

whether working capital  
can support inventory expansion

quizá sea secundario.

---

# **37\. Evidence Hierarchy Is Contextual**

No existe una fuente universalmente superior para toda pregunta.

---

# **38\. Source Conflict**

Puede ocurrir:

Shopify revenue says X

Finance says Y

Ad platform says Z

---

# **39\. Conflict Must Be Visible**

No seleccionar silenciosamente el número conveniente.

---

# **40\. Data Conflict Creates Decision Uncertainty**

Puede requerir:

RECONCILIATION

antes de ranking de alta exposición.

---

# **41\. Fact**

Conceptualmente:

FACT

es algo suficientemente soportado por evidencia disponible.

---

# **42\. Inference**

INFERENCE

es una interpretación derivada de hechos.

---

# **43\. Assumption**

ASSUMPTION

es algo que necesitamos considerar pero no está plenamente demostrado.

---

# **44\. Preference**

PREFERENCE

expresa qué resultado desea o protege el negocio.

---

# **45\. Prediction**

PREDICTION

estima un estado futuro incierto.

---

# **46\. Recommendation**

RECOMMENDATION

propone qué merece precedencia dados los anteriores.

---

# **47\. These Must Not Be Blended**

Ejemplo incorrecto:

Germany will be profitable  
and therefore expansion is priority.

Puede haber fusionado:

ASSUMPTION  
\+  
PREDICTION  
\+  
RECOMMENDATION

como si fueran un hecho.

---

# **48\. Better**

Current comparable-market evidence  
suggests Germany may support  
attractive unit economics.

However this remains uncertain.

Given the current inventory constraint,  
expansion does not receive execution  
priority yet.

---

# **49\. Fact / Inference / Decision Chain**

Modelo:

FACT

FACT

INFERENCE  
Existing supply trajectory  
cannot sustain expected demand

DECISION  
Inventory receives high priority

# **50\. Preference Should Be Explicit**

Because the current objective  
is cash preservation...

# **51\. Otherwise Ranking Can Look Universal**

BUSINESS PRIORITIES.

# **52\. Decision Context Must Travel With Decision**

Una decisión sin contexto puede quedar obsoleta o malinterpretada.

---

# **53\. Example**

"Reduce paid acquisition."

puede ser razonable bajo:

cash preservation

pero parecer absurda meses después durante:

aggressive expansion.

---

# **54\. Decision Timestamp Matters Conceptually**

Porque:

PRIORITY(t)

cambia.

---

# **55\. “As Of” Thinking**

Una recomendación debería entenderse como:

BEST CURRENT JUDGMENT  
UNDER CURRENT INFORMATION.

# **56\. Permanent Advice Is Suspicious**

Ecommerce cambia constantemente.

# **57\. Decision Assumptions**

Toda decisión se apoya en supuestos.

Pregunta:

WHAT MUST BE TRUE  
FOR THIS DECISION  
TO REMAIN GOOD?

---

# **58\. Critical Assumptions**

No registrar todos los supuestos posibles.

Principalmente:

THE ONES THAT  
COULD CHANGE THE DECISION.

---

# **59\. Decision-Critical Assumption**

Ejemplo:

Campaign scaling is attractive  
if marginal CAC remains below X.

---

# **60\. Non-Critical Assumption**

Pequeña diferencia que no cambia ranking.

No merece misma atención.

---

# **61\. Assumption Load**

Una decisión basada en:

1 strong assumption

no es igual a otra basada en:

8 uncertain assumptions.

---

# **62\. Assumption Stacking**

Varias condiciones inciertas necesarias simultáneamente aumentan fragilidad.

---

# **63\. Explain Assumption Fragility**

Ejemplo:

Expansion ranks fourth primarily because  
its upside depends simultaneously on  
unvalidated CAC, repeat behavior and  
local return economics.

# **64\. Unknown**

UNKNOWN.

# **66\. Unknown Can Be Decision-Relevant**

Ejemplo:

unknown stock availability

# **67\. Known Unknown**

# **68\. Unknown Unknown**

# **69\. Uncertainty Communication**

# **70\. Confidence**

Conceptualmente:

DECISION CONFIDENCE

es:

> **El grado de soporte que existe para preferir la decisión actual frente a alternativas relevantes.**

---

# **71\. Decision Confidence ≠ Diagnostic Confidence**

Podemos estar muy seguros del problema y poco seguros de qué debe ir primero.

---

# **72\. Example**

Pero:

Should retention outrank inventory?

puede tener baja decision confidence.

---

# **73\. Ranking Confidence**

A ≈ B.

# **75\. Better Confidence Explanation**

# **76\. Confidence Calibration**

CERTAINTY THEATRE.

---

# **77\. Linguistic Confidence ≠ Evidential Confidence**

Que la IA escriba con seguridad:

does not increase evidence.

---

# **78\. Excessive Hedging Is Also Bad**

No todo necesita:

maybe

si evidencia es fuerte.

# **79\. Calibrated Communication**

# **80\. Confidence Should Affect Commitment**

# **81\. High Priority / Low Confidence**

# **83\. Low Priority / Low Confidence**

Puede justificar:

MONITOR

# **84\. Explanation of Rejected Alternatives**

WHY NOT B?

# **86\. Why Not the Runner-Up?**

DID THE SYSTEM

# **89\. But Do Not Explain Every Rejected Candidate**

MATERIALLY RELEVANT

# **90\. Dominated Alternatives**

P10.

# **91\. Close Alternatives**

WHAT HAPPENS

A creates more relevant value

# **97\. This Makes Scarcity Visible**

# **102\. Avoid Misinterpreting Sequence**

# **104\. Different Decision Types Need Different Explanations**

# **132\. Validation ≠ “Did Revenue Go Up?”**

UNCERTAINTY EXISTS.

---

# **599\. Principle of Explainable Priority**

> **A priority should never be presented merely as “the answer”. It should be presented as the best current decision under a specific context, supported by specific evidence, dependent on explicit assumptions and open to revision when material facts change.**

---

# **600\. Principle of Validation**

> **A decision should not be judged solely by whether the eventual outcome was good or bad, but by whether the decision was reasonable given the information available at the time, whether the expected mechanism actually occurred and what new evidence the outcome provides.**

---

# **601\. Principle of Traceability**

> **Every material recommendation should be reconstructable from business context → evidence → interpretation → trade-off → priority.**

---

# **602\. Principle of Uncertainty**

> **Uncertainty should not disappear merely because a recommendation must be produced.**

---

# **603\. Principle of Revision**

> **Changing a recommendation in response to materially changed evidence is not inconsistency; refusing to change it is.**

---

# **604\. Principle of Stability**

> **At the same time, a priority should not change merely because a noisy metric moved yesterday.**

---

# **605\. Principle of Learning**

> **The purpose of reviewing a decision is not to assign blame to a winner or loser, but to improve the model that produces the next decision.**

---

# **606\. Core Final Question**

The complete P12 question is:

> **¿Podemos explicar exactamente qué estamos priorizando, por qué merece precedencia ahora, qué evidencia y supuestos soportan esa conclusión, qué alternativa estamos sacrificando, qué incertidumbre permanece, qué hecho nos haría cambiar de opinión y cómo sabremos posteriormente si el razonamiento decisional fue correcto?**

---
