- RAG: semantic distinction.
- Contracts: interaction type.

- Learning: conflict frequency.

- RAG: global resource reasoning.
- Contracts: capacity/reservation.

- Tenant: actual resources.

### Lock
- RAG: coordination mechanism concept.
- Contracts: owner/scope/mode.
- Runtime: acquire/release/wait.
- Policies: minimum sufficient lock.
- Tenant: granularity/order.
- Learning: wait/duration quality.

### Priority / Preemption
- RAG: priority logic.
- Contracts: basis and ownership.
- Runtime: scheduling/preemption.
- Policies: safety > optimization.
- Tenant: business policy.
- Learning: outcomes.

### Dependency
- RAG: intervention graph.
- Contracts: upstream/downstream.

### Admission Control
- RAG: local-ready can still be globally inadmissible.
- Contracts: admit/queue/merge/defer/reject.
- Runtime: entry flow.
- Tenant: concrete rules.

### Supersession
- RAG: new plan can replace old.
- Contracts: old/new relation.
- Runtime: stale plan prevention.
- Policies: no hidden supersession.
- Learning: stale execution incidents.

### Portfolio Risk
- RAG: aggregate/global view.
- Contracts: portfolio state.
- Runtime: monitoring/safe mode.
- Policies: aggregate limits.
- Tenant: thresholds.
- Learning: risk concentration.

### Deadlock / Livelock / Starvation
- RAG: concepts.
- Contracts: representable coordination failure.
- Runtime: detection/resolution.
- Policies: no indefinite silent wait.
- Learning: patterns.

## Redundancia consolidada

El documento repite de forma útil:

- concurrency is not conflict;
- local correctness is not global correctness;
- global limits matter;
- revalidate before write;
- parallelize independent work;
- serialize conflicting work;
- waiting is not failure;
- recovery overrides optimization;
- conflicts must be explicit;
- revalidate after waiting.

Estas ideas se consolidaron en `01-rag-produccion`.
