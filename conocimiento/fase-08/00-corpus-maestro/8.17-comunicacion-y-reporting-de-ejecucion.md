# **FASE 8 — EJECUCIÓN Y SUPERVISIÓN**

## **8.17 Comunicación y reporting de ejecución**

---

# **1\. Objetivo del bloque**

Una IA operacional no solo debe:

decidir  
ejecutar  
supervisar  
recuperar

También debe comunicar correctamente lo que está ocurriendo.

La pregunta central es:

> **¿Qué debe informar la IA durante, después o ante una incidencia?**

Una ejecución técnicamente buena puede convertirse en una mala experiencia operacional si:

nadie sabe qué está ocurriendo  
las alertas llegan tarde  
el humano recibe demasiado ruido  
las decisiones necesarias no están claras

---

# **2\. Communication Is Part of Execution**

La comunicación no debe tratarse como una capa decorativa.

Forma parte del sistema de control.

Conceptualmente:

EXECUTION  
\+  
OBSERVABILITY  
\+  
COMMUNICATION  
\=  
OPERABLE SYSTEM

---

# **3\. Por qué importa**

Un humano necesita saber, según contexto:

qué está ocurriendo  
qué cambió  
qué riesgo existe  
qué decisión necesita tomar  
qué ha terminado

---

# **4\. Reporting ≠ Logging**

Un log contiene eventos.

Un report transforma eventos en información útil.

Ejemplo de log:

Campaign update completed at 10:05:32.

Ejemplo de reporting:

Campaign A increased from €500/day to €525/day.  
Change verified successfully.

---

# **5\. Logging sirve a sistemas**

Reporting sirve a decisiones.

---

# **6\. Communication Audience**

No todos necesitan la misma información.

Puede existir:

Operator  
Manager  
Executive  
Specialist  
Customer-facing team

---

# **7\. Audience-Aware Communication**

La misma intervención puede necesitar diferentes niveles.

---

# **8\. Operator View**

Necesita:

current state  
active step  
errors  
next action

---

# **9\. Manager View**

Necesita:

progress  
risk  
impact  
decisions required

---

# **10\. Executive View**

Normalmente:

business impact  
critical risk  
status

sin microdetalle.

---

# **11\. Specialist View**

Puede necesitar:

technical symptoms  
affected systems  
failure evidence

---

# **12\. Communication Principle**

> **Comunicar la mínima cantidad de información necesaria para que el receptor entienda, decida o actúe correctamente.**

---

# **13\. Too Little Communication**

Produce:

blindness  
surprises  
poor trust  
late intervention

---

# **14\. Too Much Communication**

Produce:

noise  
alert fatigue  
decision fatigue  
ignored notifications

---

# **15\. Signal-to-Noise Ratio**

El reporting debe maximizar:

useful signal  
/  
total communication

---

# **16\. Communication Types**

Una ejecución puede generar:

STATUS  
PROGRESS  
APPROVAL REQUEST  
WARNING  
INCIDENT  
ESCALATION  
RECOVERY  
CLOSURE

---

# **17\. Status**

Responde:

> ¿En qué estado está?

Ejemplo:

RUNNING  
PAUSED  
BLOCKED  
VERIFYING  
CLOSED

---

# **18\. Progress**

Responde:

> ¿Cuánto ha avanzado?

---

# **19\. Approval Request**

Responde:

> ¿Qué decisión humana necesitamos antes de continuar?

---

# **20\. Warning**

Responde:

> ¿Qué señal preocupante apareció aunque todavía no exista incidente crítico?

---

# **21\. Incident Communication**

Responde:

> ¿Qué salió mal, qué impacto tiene y qué estamos haciendo?

---

# **22\. Escalation Communication**

Responde:

> ¿Qué decisión supera la autonomía de la IA?

---

# **23\. Recovery Communication**

Responde:

> ¿Estamos recuperando y cuál es el estado actual?

---

# **24\. Closure Communication**

Responde:

> ¿Qué terminó, qué resultado quedó y qué sigue pendiente?

---

# **25\. Event ≠ Notification**

No todo evento debe generar una notificación.

---

# **26\. Example**

Eventos internos:

step started  
step completed  
metric read

pueden registrarse sin interrumpir a nadie.

---

# **27\. Notification Threshold**

Solo debería notificarse cuando:

human awareness  
or  
human action

aporta valor.

---

# **28\. Informational Message**

No requiere respuesta.

Ejemplo:

Rollout stage 1 completed and verified.

---

# **29\. Actionable Message**

Requiere decisión.

Ejemplo:

Campaign scaling paused.  
Approval required to resume above €600/day.

---

# **30\. Critical Message**

Requiere atención inmediata.

Ejemplo:

Refund automation stopped after duplicate transaction detection.

---

# **31\. Severity**

La comunicación puede clasificarse:

INFO  
NOTICE  
WARNING  
HIGH  
CRITICAL

conceptualmente.

---

# **32\. INFO**

Información rutinaria.

---

# **33\. NOTICE**

Cambio relevante pero sin riesgo inmediato.

---

# **34\. WARNING**

Existe deterioro o riesgo creciente.

---

# **35\. HIGH**

Existe problema material.

---

# **36\. CRITICAL**

Existe:

active severe risk

o necesidad urgente de intervención.

---

# **37\. Severity Must Affect Delivery**

No todas las severidades deben utilizar los mismos canales o prioridad.

---

# **38\. Critical Must Stand Out**

Si una alerta crítica parece igual que:

"campaign updated successfully"

el sistema está mal diseñado.

---

# **39\. Alert Fatigue**

Si todo es crítico:

nothing is critical

---

# **40\. Escalation Fatigue**

Mismo principio.

---

# **41\. Communication Timing**

Una comunicación puede ocurrir:

before execution  
during execution  
after execution  
after outcome observation

---

# **42\. Pre-Execution Communication**

Puede informar:

what will happen  
scope  
risk  
approval required

---

# **43\. During Execution Communication**

Debe centrarse en:

progress  
deviation  
risk  
decisions needed

---

# **44\. Post-Execution Communication**

Debe explicar:

what happened  
verification  
final state

---

# **45\. Outcome Communication**

Puede llegar después.

Ejemplo:

72h outcome:  
Revenue \+8 %, CPA stable.

---

# **46\. Communication Cadence**

No existe una única frecuencia correcta.

Depende de:

risk  
duration  
velocity  
audience

---

# **47\. Short Intervention**

Puede necesitar:

start  
end

solamente.

---

# **48\. Long Intervention**

Puede necesitar:

periodic updates

---

# **49\. High-Risk Intervention**

Puede requerir reporting frecuente.

---

# **50\. Low-Risk Background Work**

Debe evitar exceso de mensajes.

---

# **51\. Event-Driven Communication**

Un evento importante dispara comunicación.

Ejemplo:

guardrail breached

---

# **52\. Periodic Communication**

Puede resumir progreso.

---

# **53\. State-Transition Communication**

Algunas transiciones deberían comunicarse.

Ejemplo:

RUNNING  
→ PAUSED

---

# **54\. Not Every State Transition Needs Human Message**

Ejemplo interno:

VERIFYING  
→ VERIFIED

puede incorporarse al cierre.

---

# **55\. Material Transition**

Debe comunicar si cambia:

risk  
responsibility  
progress  
decision requirements

---

# **56\. Message Structure**

Una buena comunicación operacional suele responder:

WHAT  
WHY  
CURRENT STATE  
IMPACT  
NEXT STEP

---

# **57\. WHAT**

¿Qué ocurrió?

---

# **58\. WHY**

¿Por qué ocurrió o por qué cambió comportamiento?

---

# **59\. CURRENT STATE**

¿Dónde estamos ahora?

---

# **60\. IMPACT**

¿Qué significa?

---

# **61\. NEXT STEP**

¿Qué ocurrirá o qué debe decidir alguien?

---

# **62\. Example**

Campaign A has been paused  
because stock fell below 200 units.

Current spend exposure is stopped.

The intervention will remain paused  
until inventory is replenished or a manager overrides the hold.

---

# **63\. Good Reporting Is Decision-Oriented**

No debe obligar al usuario a preguntar:

"So what?"

---

# **64\. State-First Reporting**

Puede ser útil comenzar:

PAUSED — Stock guardrail triggered.

cuando lo más relevante sea el estado.

---

# **65\. Impact-First Reporting**

En incidentes:

12 duplicate refunds detected.  
Refund automation has been stopped.

---

# **66\. Decision-First Reporting**

Cuando necesita humano:

Decision required:  
Resume Campaign A at €500/day or keep it paused?

---

# **67\. Executive Summary**

Puede resumir:

status  
impact  
decision

en pocas líneas.

---

# **68\. Detail on Demand**

El detalle puede estar disponible sin saturar el resumen.

---

# **69\. Layered Reporting**

Conceptualmente:

SUMMARY  
↓  
DETAILS  
↓  
EVIDENCE

---

# **70\. Summary Layer**

Para decisión rápida.

---

# **71\. Detail Layer**

Para comprensión operacional.

---

# **72\. Evidence Layer**

Para auditoría y debugging.

---

# **73\. Avoid Raw Tool Dumps**

Ejemplo malo:

HTTP 200  
job id 38292  
status ok

si el usuario necesita saber:

Campaign A budget updated successfully.

---

# **74\. Translate Tool Semantics Into Business Semantics**

Muy importante.

---

# **75\. Tool Error Translation**

No solo:

403

sino:

The agent no longer has permission  
to modify campaign budgets.

---

# **76\. Technical Detail When Useful**

Puede añadirse para especialistas.

---

# **77\. Audience-Specific Detail**

Manager:

Campaign update blocked by missing permission.

Engineer:

Write permission missing on Ads account.

---

# **78\. Progress Reporting**

Debe responder:

how much done?  
what remains?

---

# **79\. Example**

Pricing rollout:  
75/100 SKUs verified.  
25 remain.

---

# **80\. Stage Reporting**

Puede ser:

Pilot complete.  
Rollout stage 2 started.

---

# **81\. ETA Caution**

No debería inventar tiempos si no puede estimarlos con fiabilidad.

Mejor:

Waiting for external sync.

que prometer una hora arbitraria.

---

# **82\. Progress ≠ Activity**

Un sistema puede realizar muchas acciones sin avanzar.

---

# **83\. Report Useful Progress**

No:

500 tool calls completed.

sino:

80 % of catalog verified.

---

# **84\. Milestones**

Los hitos son mejores puntos de comunicación que cada microacción.

---

# **85\. Milestone Examples**

pilot complete  
approval received  
50 % rollout reached  
verification completed

---

# **86\. Change Reporting**

Cuando se modifica un plan, debe comunicarse:

what changed  
why

---

# **87\. Example**

Rollout reduced from 20 % to 5 %  
after CPA entered the warning zone.

---

# **88\. Plan Drift Reporting**

Si hubo desviación material:

report it

---

# **89\. Do Not Hide Adaptive Changes**

Aunque estuvieran permitidos.

---

# **90\. Approval Request Reporting**

Debe incluir:

decision  
context  
risk  
recommended option

---

# **91\. Bad Approval Request**

Approve?

---

# **92\. Better Approval Request**

Approve increasing Campaign A  
from €500/day to €600/day?

Expected upside:  
more conversion volume.

Main risk:  
stock currently covers approximately 4 days.

---

# **93\. Approval Scope**

Debe quedar claro qué se está aprobando.

---

# **94\. Approval Duration**

Si aplica:

for this stage  
for 24 hours  
for this campaign

---

# **95\. No Ambiguous Approval**

Evitar:

"Approve scaling"

cuando existen muchas campañas.

---

# **96\. Approval Diff**

Es útil mostrar:

CURRENT  
→  
PROPOSED

---

# **97\. Example**

Budget:  
€500/day  
→ €600/day

---

# **98\. Warning Communication**

Debe indicar:

warning signal  
current value  
threshold  
current response

---

# **99\. Example**

Warning:  
CPA reached €58 against  
a €60 intervention threshold.

No additional scaling will occur  
until performance stabilizes.

---

# **100\. Warning ≠ Incident**

No debería usar lenguaje alarmista innecesario.

---

# **101\. Incident Reporting**

En incidente debe responder inmediatamente:

what happened  
what is affected  
is damage still active  
what containment happened

---

# **102\. Example**

Critical incident:

Duplicate refunds were detected  
on 3 orders.

Refund automation has been disabled.  
No additional refunds are being processed.

Finance review is required.

---

# **103\. Incident Reporting Priority**

Primero:

IMPACT  
\+  
CONTAINMENT

Después detalles.

---

# **104\. Do Not Lead With Root Cause Speculation**

Ejemplo malo:

Probably caused by synchronization issue...

antes de informar que hay dinero en riesgo.

---

# **105\. Known vs Suspected Cause**

Debe diferenciar:

KNOWN

de:

SUSPECTED

---

# **106\. Example**

Cause not yet confirmed.  
Initial evidence points to duplicated retry behavior.

---

# **107\. Avoid False Certainty**

Especialmente durante incidentes.

---

# **108\. Incident Updates**

Una incidencia larga necesita updates solo cuando cambia algo material.

---

# **109\. Material Update**

Ejemplos:

scope expanded  
containment succeeded  
cause identified  
recovery started

---

# **110\. No Repetitive Incident Spam**

No repetir:

incident still open

cada minuto sin cambio.

---

# **111\. Incident Timeline Reporting**

Puede resumir:

10:02 detected  
10:03 writes stopped  
10:08 affected orders identified

---

# **112\. Recovery Reporting**

Debe indicar:

what is being restored  
what is already safe  
what remains

---

# **113\. Example**

Recovery in progress.

18 of 20 products restored.  
2 require manual review.

Storefront remains safe and  
the affected campaign is still paused.

---

# **114\. Recovery Complete**

No decir:

fixed

sin verificar.

Mejor:

Recovery completed and verified.

---

# **115\. Partial Recovery Reporting**

Debe ser explícito.

---

# **116\. Residual Risk Reporting**

Ejemplo:

Checkout functionality is restored.

Analytics attribution remains degraded.

---

# **117\. Handoff Communication**

Debe indicar:

who owns next step

---

# **118\. Example**

Execution is complete.

The remaining analytics discrepancy  
has been handed off to the Data team.

---

# **119\. Human Takeover Reporting**

Debe decir:

AI writes suspended.  
Human control active.

---

# **120\. Avoid Control Ambiguity**

No puede haber dudas sobre quién está ejecutando.

---

# **121\. Resume Reporting**

Ejemplo:

AI execution resumed after  
human approval and readiness revalidation.

---

# **122\. Closure Reporting**

Debe responder:

what was done  
what result  
what remains

---

# **123\. Example**

Intervention closed.

Campaign A increased from €500/day  
to €525/day and the change was verified.

72-hour business outcome remains under observation.

---

# **124\. Closure Outcome Language**

Debe diferenciar:

execution success

de:

business success

---

# **125\. Example**

No:

Scaling successful.

si solo sabemos que budget cambió.

Mejor:

Budget increase executed and verified.  
Performance outcome is still being evaluated.

---

# **126\. Report Business Outcome Separately**

Cuando exista evidencia.

---

# **127\. Example**

7-day outcome:

Revenue \+12 %  
CPA \+1 %  
Contribution margin unchanged.

---

# **128\. Avoid Vanity Reporting**

No destacar únicamente métricas positivas.

---

# **129\. Balanced Outcome Reporting**

Ejemplo:

Revenue \+20 %  
but contribution margin \-8 %.

---

# **130\. Counter-Metric Reporting**

Debe incluir efectos secundarios relevantes.

---

# **131\. Reporting Against Baseline**

Mejor:

CPA moved from €42 to €48

que:

CPA is €48

si baseline importa.

---

# **132\. Reporting Against Target**

Puede mostrar:

actual vs target

---

# **133\. Reporting Against Guardrail**

También:

current vs safety limit

---

# **134\. Three-Way View**

Ejemplo:

Baseline CPA: €45  
Current CPA: €52  
Guardrail: €60

Muy informativo.

---

# **135\. Trend Reporting**

No solo snapshot.

Ejemplo:

CPA:  
€45 → €48 → €52

---

# **136\. But Avoid Data Dump**

Mostrar tendencias solo si cambian interpretación.

---

# **137\. Uncertainty Reporting**

La IA debería declarar incertidumbre relevante.

---

# **138\. Example**

Current performance looks positive,  
but sample size is still limited.

---

# **139\. Confidence Reporting**

Puede indicar:

HIGH  
MEDIUM  
LOW

conceptualmente cuando aporte valor.

---

# **140\. Confidence ≠ Probability Theater**

No inventar porcentajes arbitrarios.

---

# **141\. Evidence-Based Confidence**

Debe relacionarse con:

data quality  
sample  
verification  
consistency

---

# **142\. Missing Data Reporting**

Ejemplo:

Revenue data available.  
Margin data currently unavailable.

---

# **143\. No Silent Missing Metrics**

Especialmente si cambian decisión.

---

# **144\. Observability Failure Reporting**

Debe informar cuando:

we can no longer monitor safely

---

# **145\. Example**

Purchase tracking is unavailable.

Autonomous campaign scaling has been paused  
because ROAS can no longer be monitored reliably.

---

# **146\. Reporting Context Changes**

Ejemplo:

Stock dropped from 600 to 180 units  
during the intervention.

---

# **147\. Explain Behavioral Change**

Entonces:

Scaling stopped.

---

# **148\. Cause-Effect Reporting**

Siempre que sea cierto:

signal  
→ response

---

# **149\. Do Not Overstate Causality**

Para business outcomes:

after

no significa siempre:

because of

---

# **150\. Operational Causality vs Business Causality**

Sí podemos decir:

Campaign paused because stock guardrail triggered.

Pero no necesariamente:

Revenue increased because campaign budget changed.

sin evidencia suficiente.

---

# **151\. Reporting Concurrent Interventions**

Cuando afectan interpretación:

another pricing experiment was active

debe indicarse.

---

# **152\. Confounder Reporting**

Especialmente para Fase 9\.

---

# **153\. Example**

Conversion increased 8 % during the test,  
but a sitewide promotion ran simultaneously.

---

# **154\. Honest Interpretation**

Esto mejora aprendizaje.

---

# **155\. Communication of Blocked State**

Debe explicar:

what is blocking  
what resolves it

---

# **156\. Example**

Execution blocked.

Reason:  
Shopify write permission is unavailable.

Next:  
An administrator must restore access.

---

# **157\. Blocked ≠ Error Language**

No decir:

execution failed

si solo espera approval.

---

# **158\. Communication of Waiting**

Ejemplo:

Waiting for human approval.  
No changes have been applied.

---

# **159\. Waiting Safely**

Puede indicar:

current state remains unchanged

---

# **160\. Communication of Hold**

Rollout held at 20 % exposure  
while performance stabilizes.

---

# **161\. Hold ≠ Pause**

Debe distinguirse.

---

# **162\. Communication of Pause**

Execution paused.  
No additional changes will be applied.

---

# **163\. Communication of Abort**

Intervention aborted.  
No further rollout will occur.

---

# **164\. Communication of Rollback**

Rollback started:  
restoring the previous campaign budget.

---

# **165\. Rollback Completed**

Previous state restored and verified.

---

# **166\. Rollback Failed**

Debe ser explícito.

Rollback failed.  
The affected state remains inconsistent.  
Human intervention is required.

---

# **167\. Communication of Supersession**

This intervention was cancelled because  
a newer inventory-protection intervention superseded it.

---

# **168\. Not Failure**

Debe evitarse interpretarlo como fallo.

---

# **169\. Communication of Expiration**

Ejemplo:

Intervention expired before execution  
because the promotional window ended.

---

# **170\. Communication of Unknown State**

Muy importante.

Refund request outcome is currently unknown.  
No retry will occur until the transaction state is verified.

---

# **171\. Unknown Must Be Visible**

No ocultarlo tras:

processing

indefinidamente.

---

# **172\. Communication During Ambiguity**

Debe proteger contra acciones humanas duplicadas.

Ejemplo:

Do not issue a manual refund yet;  
the original transaction is still being reconciled.

---

# **173\. Coordination Communication**

Cuando una intervención espera otra:

Pricing rollout queued until  
the current checkout recovery is complete.

---

# **174\. Conflict Communication**

Ejemplo:

Two active interventions require  
incompatible changes to Campaign A.  
Execution has been held pending resolution.

---

# **175\. Shared Resource Communication**

Global ad-spend capacity is fully reserved  
by other active interventions.

---

# **176\. Queue Communication**

No hace falta notificar cada movimiento de cola.

Solo cuando relevante.

---

# **177\. Priority Change Reporting**

Si cambia materialmente:

Inventory recovery promoted to highest priority  
after stock synchronization failed globally.

---

# **178\. Reporting Human Override**

Debe distinguirse del comportamiento autónomo.

---

# **179\. Example**

Campaign A was resumed by a manager override.

---

# **180\. Why Attribution Matters**

Para auditoría y Fase 9\.

---

# **181\. Reporting AI Decision**

Ejemplo:

AI paused rollout automatically  
after the predefined margin guardrail was breached.

---

# **182\. Reporting Policy-Driven Action**

También distinguirlo.

---

# **183\. Actor Attribution**

Puede ser:

AI  
Human  
Policy  
External System

---

# **184\. No Blame-Language by Default**

Informar hechos.

No:

Marketing broke campaign X.

---

# **185\. Neutral Operational Language**

Mejor:

Campaign X budget was manually changed  
during the active intervention.

---

# **186\. Reporting Failures**

Debe ser preciso:

what failed  
what did not fail

---

# **187\. Example**

3 of 100 product updates failed.  
The remaining 97 were applied and verified.

---

# **188\. Avoid Catastrophic Wording for Partial Failure**

No:

Catalog update failed.

si 97 % funcionó.

---

# **189\. But Avoid Minimizing Critical Failure**

Si 3 fallos afectan:

payment SKUs

pueden ser materialmente críticos.

---

# **190\. Contextual Severity Reporting**

No solo porcentaje.

---

# **191\. Report Impact, Not Just Count**

Ejemplo:

3 failed updates affect 42 % of product revenue.

---

# **192\. Materiality**

Una pequeña cantidad puede tener gran impacto.

---

# **193\. Closure Reporting Levels**

Puede existir:

operational summary  
business summary  
learning summary

---

# **194\. Operational Summary**

Qué se ejecutó.

---

# **195\. Business Summary**

Qué impacto observamos.

---

# **196\. Learning Summary**

Qué debería analizar Fase 9\.

---

# **197\. Learning Reporting Must Avoid Premature Conclusions**

Puede decir:

Initial signal suggests...

---

# **198\. Outcome Maturity**

Indicar:

EARLY  
MATURE  
INCOMPLETE

si relevante.

---

# **199\. Example**

Early outcome:  
CVR \+6 % after 48 hours.  
Evidence is not yet mature enough  
for a permanent recommendation.

---

# **200\. Reporting Interventions With No Business Outcome Yet**

Debe decirlo claramente.

---

# **201\. Example**

Execution completed and verified.  
Business outcome requires a 7-day observation window.

---

# **202\. Reporting Rejected Recommendations**

Ejemplo:

Recommendation not executed.

required margin threshold was not met.

---

# **203\. Reporting Blocked Recommendations**

También útil.

---

Una guardrail que evita un cambio debería aparecer como control exitoso cuando material.

---

# **205\. Example**

---

# **206\. Blocked Unsafe Action ≠ Failure**

Es:

governance success

---

# **207\. Reporting No-Op**

A veces la acción ya estaba satisfecha.

---

# **208\. Example**

No change applied.  
Campaign A was already paused.

---

# **209\. No-Op Is Useful**

Evita incertidumbre.

---

# **210\. Reporting Deduplicated Actions**

Duplicate execution request detected.  
No second refund was issued.

---

# **211\. Safety Actions Are Worth Reporting When Material**

---

# **212\. Reporting Policy Conflicts**

Ejemplo:

Requested discount conflicts with the current margin floor.  
The action has been blocked.

---

# **213\. Reporting Authority Limits**

The requested refund exceeds the agent's autonomous authority.  
Finance approval is required.

# **214\. Explain Boundary, Not Internal Mechanics**

El usuario necesita entender el motivo.

---

# **215\. Reporting Tool Degradation**

No hace falta avisar de todo timeout.

# **216\. Notify When It Changes Execution**

Ejemplo:

Ads integration is degraded.

Debe ser accionable.

# **803\. Modelo mental universal**

Antes de comunicar algo, la IA debería preguntarse:

1\. ¿Quién necesita saber esto?

2\. ¿Es información o requiere acción?

3\. ¿Cuál es la severidad real?

4\. ¿Qué ocurrió?

5\. ¿Qué sabemos con certeza?

6\. ¿Qué sigue siendo desconocido?

7\. ¿Qué impacto existe?

8\. ¿El riesgo sigue activo?

9\. ¿Qué acción ya se tomó?

10\. ¿Qué decisión falta?

11\. ¿Quién debe tomarla?

12\. ¿Cuál es el estado actual real?

13\. ¿La información está actualizada?

14\. ¿Estoy comunicando demasiado ruido?

# **805\. Regla central de 8.17**

La regla fundamental es:

> **La IA debe comunicar cuando la información cambia la comprensión, la decisión o la capacidad de control de un humano; el resto puede permanecer como trazabilidad interna.**

En forma resumida:

EVENT  
↓  
IS IT MATERIAL?  
├─ NO → TRACE ONLY  
└─ YES  
    ↓  
DOES HUMAN NEED TO KNOW?  
├─ NO → INTERNAL REPORTING  
└─ YES  
    ↓  
DOES HUMAN NEED TO ACT?  
├─ NO → INFORM  
└─ YES → DECISION-READY COMMUNICATION

# **806\. Principio final**

Una IA operacional excelente no debería hablar constantemente.

Tampoco debería operar en silencio.

Debe saber distinguir:

lo que debe quedar registrado,  
lo que debe ser informado,  
lo que requiere atención,  
y lo que requiere una decisión humana.

El objetivo es que un responsable pueda entender rápidamente:

qué está pasando  
qué riesgo existe  
qué ha hecho la IA  
qué necesita de mí

sin tener que reconstruir cientos de eventos técnicos.
