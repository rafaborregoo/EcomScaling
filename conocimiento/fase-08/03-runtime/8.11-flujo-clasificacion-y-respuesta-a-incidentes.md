# FASE 8 — EJECUCIÓN Y SUPERVISIÓN
## 8.11 — Flujo de clasificación y respuesta a incidentes

### Propósito

Este runtime evita respuestas indiscriminadas y selecciona una respuesta proporcional al tipo de problema.

---

## 1. Flujo principal

1. detect problem;
2. determine whether state is known;
3. classify type;
4. assess severity;
5. assess urgency;
6. assess scope;
7. assess recoverability;
8. assess propagation risk;
9. decide local handling vs incident mode;
10. contain if needed;
11. diagnose;
12. recover;
13. verify;
14. close or escalate.

---

## 2. Unknown First

Si no se conoce si una acción ocurrió:

- mark UNKNOWN;
- stop unsafe retry;
- verify current state.

---

## 3. Error Classification

Clasificar como:

- technical;
- business;
- authorization;
- data;
- dependency;
- policy;
- concurrency;
- unknown.

---

## 4. Recoverable Error

Si es transitorio y retry-safe:

- wait;
- verify;
- bounded retry.

---

## 5. Non-Recoverable Error

Si permission/policy/target invalid:

- do not retry loop;
- block;
- escalate/replan as needed.

---

## 6. Expected Exception

Si la condición está prevista:

- skip safely;
- record;
- continue if plan remains valid.

---

## 7. Unexpected Exception

- reduce autonomy;
- inspect;
- escalate if material.

---

## 8. Blocker

- set BLOCKED;
- identify dependency/owner;
- wait;
- revalidate after resolution.

---

## 9. Degradation

- lower rate;
- reduce scope;
- increase monitoring;
- continue only if safe.

---

## 10. Action Failure

- isolate failed action;
- determine criticality;
- retry/repair/skip/escalate.

---

## 11. Intervention Failure

If critical path cannot succeed:

- stop normal progression;
- recover/rollback/abort/escalate.

---

## 12. Severity Assessment

Use:

- impact;
- scope;
- financial exposure;
- customer exposure;
- reversibility;
- duration;
- propagation;
- criticality.

---

## 13. Urgency Assessment

Consider:

- time-to-damage;
- active harm;
- containment latency.

---

## 14. Local vs Global

If localized:

- isolate affected domain.

If global/systemic:

- wider containment.

---

## 15. Failure Clustering

Detect concentration by:

- tool;
- market;
- product group;
- dependency.

---

## 16. Common Cause

If correlated failures:

- investigate shared dependency;
- avoid treating each as isolated.

---

## 17. Containment

For active material risk:

- pause;
- disable;
- isolate;
- limit;
- stop expansion.

---

## 18. Incident Mode

Switch from NORMAL to INCIDENT MODE.

Permit only:

- observe;
- contain;
- diagnose;
- recover;
- communicate.

---

## 19. Triage

Produce:

- type;
- severity;
- urgency;
- affected scope;
- immediate next action;
- owner.

---

## 20. Escalation

Escalate when:

- authority insufficient;
- critical system involved;
- state unknown;
- persistent failure;
- containment fails;
- risk too high.

---

## 21. Retry

Before retry:

- verify current state;
- verify idempotency;
- verify active guardrails;
- verify retry budget.

---

## 22. Retry Exhaustion

Transition to:

- blocked;
- failed;
- escalated;

depending on context.

---

## 23. Partial Failure

Identify:

- succeeded;
- failed;
- unknown;
- consistency risk.

Then choose:

- retry failed subset;
- rollback successful subset;
- repair;
- accept degraded;
- escalate.

---

## 24. Cascading Failure

If propagation detected:

- broaden containment enough to stop cascade;
- not more than necessary.

---

## 25. Fault Isolation

Keep safe unaffected capabilities operational when possible.

---

## 26. Graceful Degradation

If writes unsafe but reads healthy:

- writes off;
- reads/monitoring on.

---

## 27. Failover

Use alternative only if:

- authorized;
- same/lower authority;
- semantically equivalent;
- acceptable safety.

---

## 28. Human Fallback

If no safe machine path:

- handoff;
- provide incident context package.

---

## 29. Severity Reassessment

Update classification when new evidence appears.

---

## 30. Temporary Mitigation

If workaround allows operation:

- document residual risk;
- keep incident open if root cause unresolved.

---

## 31. Recovery

Choose:

- retry;
- repair;
- rollback;
- compensation;
- handoff;
- abort.

---

## 32. Recovery Verification

Do not mark resolved until safe operation is verified.

---

## 33. Incident Ownership

Assign owner and maintain ownership through handoff.

---

## 34. Safe Handoff

Until human accepts:

- containment remains active when appropriate.

---

## 35. Resolution

Check:

- impact contained;
- system safe;
- recovery verified;
- recurrence risk addressed sufficiently.

---

## 36. Closure

Close incident only after:

- resolution criteria;
- documentation;
- evidence preservation.

---

## 37. Evidence Preservation

Capture:

- current state;
- affected objects;
- recent changes;
- error/tool context.

Do not destroy useful evidence during cleanup.

---

## 38. Trust Regression

After serious incident:

- reduce autonomy;
- require approval;
- quarantine tool/capability/scope as needed.

---

## 39. Execution vs Incident State

Maintain separately.

Example:

- execution paused;
- incident contained but open.

---

## 40. Output

The runtime must answer:

- what happened;
- how severe;
- is damage active;
- can it propagate;
- is it retryable;
- what response is allowed;
- whether human escalation is required.
