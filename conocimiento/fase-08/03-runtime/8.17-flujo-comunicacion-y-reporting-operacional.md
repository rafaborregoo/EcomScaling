# FASE 8 — EJECUCIÓN Y SUPERVISIÓN
## 8.17 — Flujo de comunicación y reporting operacional

### Propósito

Este runtime decide qué eventos permanecen como trazabilidad interna y cuáles deben convertirse en comunicación humana.

---

## 1. Core Flow

1. receive execution event;
2. read authoritative current state;
3. classify materiality;
4. classify communication type;
5. classify severity/priority;
6. identify audience;
7. determine if human awareness is required;
8. determine if human action is required;
9. build message at correct detail level;
10. sanitize/privacy-check;
11. choose delivery path;
12. deliver;
13. track acknowledgement/decision when needed;
14. update/correct/resolve message lifecycle;
15. preserve trace.

---

## 2. Trace-Only Path

If event is routine and non-material:

- write internal trace;
- do not interrupt humans.

---

## 3. Informational Path

If awareness helps but action is not needed:

- build concise status/progress message;
- mark no action required where useful.

---

## 4. Decision-Required Path

If human action is needed:

- identify authorized decision owner;
- build decision-ready package;
- deliver through suitable direct channel;
- track freshness;
- track acknowledgement;
- wait for actual decision.

---

## 5. Critical Incident Path

For critical risk:

- do not delay containment for reporting;
- produce concise first alert;
- escalate immediately;
- follow with detail.

---

## 6. Message Build

Construct from:

- WHAT;
- WHY;
- CURRENT STATE;
- IMPACT;
- NEXT STEP.

---

## 7. State-First Template

Use when lifecycle state is primary.

---

## 8. Impact-First Template

Use when incident impact is primary.

---

## 9. Decision-First Template

Use when human decision is primary.

---

## 10. Audience Rendering

Render same truth differently for:

- operator;
- manager;
- executive;
- specialist.

Do not change underlying facts.

---

## 11. Layered Detail

Produce conceptually:

- summary;
- details;
- evidence.

---

## 12. Tool Error Translation

Map low-level error to:

- operational consequence;
- allowed next action.

---

## 13. Progress Compression

Aggregate microevents into:

- milestone;
- completed count;
- remaining count.

---

## 14. Milestone Triggering

Notify on configured material milestones.

---

## 15. State Transition Trigger

Evaluate transition for communication.

Not every transition sends a message.

---

## 16. Warning Trigger

When warning threshold/event occurs:

- state signal;
- state current response;
- no alarmist incident language unless severity warrants.

---

## 17. Incident Trigger

Build:

- impact;
- containment;
- known;
- unknown;
- current exposure;
- next decision.

---

## 18. Incident Updates

Update only when material state changes.

---

## 19. Incident Correlation

Group:

- warning;
- pause;
- recovery;
- resolution;

under shared incident context where appropriate.

---

## 20. Parent Incident

If common cause produces multiple symptoms:

- aggregate global summary;
- preserve affected-domain details.

---

## 21. Recovery Update

Report:

- restored scope;
- remaining scope;
- safety state;
- residual uncertainty.

---

## 22. Closure Report

Build from closure package:

- goal;
- actual execution;
- verification;
- operational outcome;
- known business outcome;
- residual risk;
- follow-up/owner.

---

## 23. Business Outcome Update

Deliver separately when mature enough.

Do not reopen execution lifecycle merely to report learning.

---

## 24. Partial State

Never collapse partial/inconclusive into success.

---

## 25. Unknown State

Report explicitly and state operational consequence.

---

## 26. Approval Request Freshness

Before presenting or accepting decision:

- re-read current state;
- compare context;
- expire stale request if needed.

---

## 27. Approval Apply Loop

For high-risk decisions:

**DECISION → APPLY → VERIFY → REPORT UPDATED STATE**

---

## 28. Deduplication

Before sending:

- detect same incident/message intent;
- update existing communication when appropriate.

---

## 29. Aggregation

Aggregate homogeneous events.

Preserve drill-down.

---

## 30. Notification Storm Control

Rate-limit/coalesce runaway notification patterns.

Do not suppress critical distinct events.

---

## 31. Acknowledgement

Track when required.

Remember:

**acknowledged ≠ approved**

---

## 32. Channel Escalation

For critical communication:

- if delivery fails/no acknowledgement and policy allows;
- use next authorized channel.

---

## 33. Communication Failure

If critical message cannot be delivered:

- raise communication risk;
- continue containment;
- escalate through fallback.

---

## 34. Correction Flow

If previous message becomes false:

- append correction;
- mark prior information superseded;
- never silently rewrite material incident history.

---

## 35. Freshness

Every current-status message should derive from current authoritative lifecycle state.

---

## 36. Restart Handling

After runtime restart:

- do not resend historical messages blindly;
- rebuild current state;
- emit only meaningful updates.

---

## 37. Notification Idempotency

Repeated event processing must not emit the same irreversible external message twice.

---

## 38. Privacy Filter

Before delivery:

- remove unnecessary personal data;
- restrict to tenant scope;
- redact secrets.

---

## 39. Role / Authority Check

Recipient may be:

- informed;
- but not authorized to decide.

Route action request only to authorized owner.

---

## 40. Decision Queue

Maintain pending human decisions separately from general activity.

Prioritize by risk/urgency.

---

## 41. Incident Queue

Maintain incident states:

- open;
- contained;
- recovering;
- resolved.

---

## 42. Attention Queue

Surface:

- decision required;
- critical warning;
- blocked requiring human;
- unresolved high-risk condition.

---

## 43. Active Execution View

Surface:

- running;
- monitoring;
- paused;
- blocked;

without conflating with attention.

---

## 44. Safe Waiting

Do not spam repeated “still waiting” messages unless a meaningful heartbeat is required.

---

## 45. Heartbeat

For long-running work:

- send periodic status only when audience benefits.

---

## 46. Risk-Based Cadence

Adjust communication intensity to:

- risk;
- intervention duration;
- velocity;
- autonomy level.

---

## 47. Earned Silence

For mature low-risk automation:

- reduce routine human messaging;
- preserve internal trace;
- maintain exception reporting.

---

## 48. No Human Action Required

Where useful, explicitly communicate that the issue is contained and no action is needed.

---

## 49. Human Takeover

On takeover:

- report AI writes suspended;
- human control active.

---

## 50. Hand-Back

On return to AI:

- revalidate state;
- report restored autonomy only after actual reactivation.

---

## 51. Reporting During Policy Enforcement

Explain:

- action blocked;
- why;
- what valid next option exists.

---

## 52. Tool/Capability Loss

Notify when it changes execution semantics.

---

## 53. Concurrency / Queue Reporting

Notify only when it materially affects:

- timing;
- risk;
- decision.

---

## 54. Portfolio Reporting

For admin-level view, aggregate:

- active interventions;
- waiting decisions;
- paused/blocked;
- portfolio risk/exposure.

---

## 55. Reporting Metrics Capture

Track conceptual timings:

- detection-to-notification;
- notification-to-acknowledgement;
- acknowledgement-to-decision;
- decision-to-action.

---

## 56. Reporting Quality Feedback

Capture human feedback where available.

---

## 57. Final Runtime Rule

**EVENT  
→ MATERIAL?  
→ HUMAN NEEDS TO KNOW?  
→ HUMAN NEEDS TO ACT?  
→ TRACE / INFORM / DECISION-READY COMMUNICATION**

All visible reporting derives from authoritative operational state.
