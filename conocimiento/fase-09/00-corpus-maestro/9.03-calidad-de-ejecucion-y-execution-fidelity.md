# **9.3 — Calidad de ejecución y Execution Fidelity**

## **¿Se ejecutó realmente la estrategia que pretendíamos evaluar?**

---

# **1\. Propósito del bloque**

Antes de aprender si una estrategia funcionó o fracasó, la IA debe responder una pregunta mucho más básica:

DID WE ACTUALLY EXECUTE  
WHAT WE INTENDED TO EXECUTE?

Este principio es crítico porque una gran cantidad de errores de aprendizaje nacen de una confusión sencilla:

PLANNED INTERVENTION  
≠  
ACTUAL EXECUTION

Si la estrategia recomendada fue:

Increase Meta budget by 20%  
over seven days  
while keeping audiences,  
creative and landing page stable.

pero en realidad ocurrió:

budget \+80%  
new audiences launched  
creative changed  
landing page modified

entonces el outcome posterior **no evalúa la recomendación original**.

Evalúa otra intervención.

Por tanto:

OUTCOME  
CAN ONLY INFORM STRATEGY QUALITY  
IF  
EXECUTION SUFFICIENTLY REPRESENTS THE STRATEGY

El objetivo del concepto **Execution Fidelity** es determinar hasta qué punto lo ejecutado coincide realmente con lo que pretendíamos evaluar.

---

# **2\. Definición de Execution Fidelity**

Execution Fidelity puede definirse conceptualmente como:

> **El grado en que la intervención realmente ejecutada conserva los elementos esenciales de la intervención planificada.**

Modelo básico:

PLANNED INTERVENTION  
↓  
ACTUAL EXECUTION  
↓  
COMPARE  
↓  
EXECUTION FIDELITY

Una fidelity alta significa:

the intended strategy  
was substantially implemented  
as intended

Una fidelity baja significa:

the observed outcome  
cannot be confidently interpreted  
as evidence about the intended strategy

---

# **3\. Execution Fidelity no significa ejecución perfecta**

Esta distinción es importante.

No necesitamos:

100% PERFECT EXECUTION

para obtener evidencia útil.

En operaciones reales habrá:

small timing deviations  
minor parameter adjustments  
human adaptations  
tool limitations  
operational imperfections

La pregunta correcta no es:

Was execution perfect?

Sino:

Did the deviations materially change  
what intervention was actually tested?

---

# **4\. Planned vs Actual**

El núcleo conceptual del bloque es:

PLANNED  
vs  
ACTUAL

Para aprender correctamente deben mantenerse separados.

Ejemplo:

## **Planned**

Send 3 lifecycle emails  
over 14 days  
to first-time customers.

## **Actual**

Email 1 sent correctly.

Email 2 delayed 6 days.

Email 3 never sent.

Audience accidentally included repeat customers.

El sistema no debería concluir:

The lifecycle flow failed.

Debería concluir primero:

The planned lifecycle flow  
was not faithfully executed.

---

# **5\. Strategy Quality vs Execution Quality**

Regla fundamental:

STRATEGY QUALITY  
≠  
EXECUTION QUALITY

Una estrategia puede ser excelente y ejecutarse mal.

También puede ocurrir lo contrario:

bad strategy  
\+  
perfect execution

El Learning Engine necesita evaluar ambas dimensiones por separado.

---

# **6\. Outcome Quality como tercera dimensión**

Debe existir una separación completa:

RECOMMENDATION QUALITY  
≠  
EXECUTION QUALITY  
≠  
OUTCOME QUALITY

Ejemplo:

Recommendation:  
Good.

Execution:  
Poor.

Outcome:  
Negative.

No podemos inferir:

Recommendation was bad.

---

# **7\. La matriz fundamental**

Existen al menos cuatro escenarios básicos.

| Estrategia | Ejecución | Resultado | Interpretación inicial |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Buena | Buena | Bueno | evidencia potencialmente favorable |
| Buena | Mala | Malo | no condenar estrategia |
| Mala | Buena | Malo | evidencia contra estrategia |
| Mala | Mala | Malo | causalidad difícil de separar |

También puede existir:

poor strategy  
\+  
poor execution  
\+  
good outcome

que podría representar suerte o confounding.

---

# **8\. Execution Fidelity como condición epistemológica**

Execution Fidelity no es solo un asunto operacional.

Es un requisito para poder aprender.

Porque:

IF WE DO NOT KNOW  
WHAT WAS ACTUALLY EXECUTED

THEN

WE DO NOT KNOW  
WHAT THE OUTCOME IS EVIDENCE ABOUT

---

# **9\. Cinco dimensiones principales de Execution Fidelity**

La base conceptual definida para esta fase requiere distinguir:

scope fidelity  
parameter fidelity  
timing fidelity  
sequence fidelity  
completion fidelity

Estas cinco dimensiones responden a diferentes tipos de desviación.

---

# **10\. Scope Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿Se aplicó la intervención al alcance correcto?

Ejemplos de scope:

customers  
segments  
channels  
markets  
products  
SKUs  
devices  
campaigns  
traffic sources

---

# **11\. Ejemplo de Scope Fidelity**

Plan:

Apply 15% discount  
to dormant customers only.

Actual:

Discount shown to all customers.

El scope cambió radicalmente.

La experiencia ya no evalúa:

reactivation discount

sino:

sitewide discount

---

# **12\. Scope leakage**

Puede existir:

SCOPE LEAKAGE

cuando la intervención alcanza unidades fuera del alcance previsto.

Ejemplo:

Test only Spain.

Pero:

promotion also activated in Portugal.

Esto puede afectar interpretación y generalización.

---

# **13\. Under-scope execution**

Caso inverso:

Plan:

all first-time buyers

Actual:

only 18% received intervention.

Aunque técnicamente algo se ejecutó, la exposición puede ser demasiado pequeña para evaluar la estrategia completa.

---

# **14\. Parameter Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿Se ejecutaron los parámetros esenciales tal como estaban definidos?

Ejemplos:

price  
discount depth  
budget  
frequency  
bid  
threshold  
audience settings  
shipping threshold  
creative volume  
email cadence

---

# **15\. Ejemplo de Parameter Fidelity**

Plan:

Increase price by 5%.

Actual:

Price increased by 20%.

Si CVR colapsa, no podemos concluir:

moderate price increases fail.

La intervención probada fue mucho más agresiva.

---

# **16\. Parameter drift**

Una intervención puede desviarse gradualmente.

Ejemplo:

Day 1:  
budget \+20%

Day 3:  
budget \+35%

Day 5:  
budget \+70%

Sin registrar este drift, podríamos pensar que evaluamos una subida controlada de 20%.

---

# **17\. Parameter fidelity and non-linearity**

Esto es especialmente importante cuando la respuesta del sistema no es lineal.

Ejemplo:

10% discount

puede mejorar conversión moderadamente.

40% discount

puede cambiar por completo:

customer mix  
margin  
brand perception  
purchase timing

No son simplemente la misma intervención “más fuerte”.

---

# **18\. Timing Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿La intervención se ejecutó en el momento previsto?

Timing puede incluir:

start date  
time of day  
day of week  
season  
campaign phase  
customer lifecycle stage  
duration

---

# **19\. Timing example**

Plan:

Launch retention email  
7 days after first purchase.

Actual:

sent after 35 days.

Mismo mensaje.

Distinta intervención.

Porque el lifecycle context cambió.

---

# **20\. Calendar timing**

Plan:

Test new pricing  
during normal trading.

Actual:

launched on Black Friday week.

Aunque el precio sea correcto, la experiencia ya está muy contaminada.

---

# **21\. Duration Fidelity**

Timing incluye también duración.

Plan:

Run test for 21 days.

Actual:

stopped after 4 days.

Un outcome negativo puede ser simplemente:

insufficient exposure

---

# **22\. Premature termination**

Las intervenciones suelen detenerse antes de madurar porque:

early metrics look weak  
stakeholder becomes nervous  
technical issue appears  
inventory changes

Esto puede sesgar el aprendizaje hacia intervenciones con efectos rápidos.

---

# **23\. Sequence Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿Los pasos se ejecutaron en el orden previsto?

Crítico en playbooks.

Ejemplo:

Plan:

1\. Fix tracking  
2\. Improve landing page  
3\. Scale paid media

Actual:

1\. Scale paid media  
2\. Improve landing page  
3\. Tracking fixed later

El resultado puede ser muy distinto.

---

# **24\. Sequence can be causal**

El orden puede cambiar:

risk  
economics  
customer response  
operational capacity

Por tanto:

SAME STEPS  
IN DIFFERENT ORDER  
≠  
SAME INTERVENTION

---

# **25\. Example — CRO before scaling**

Plan:

Improve CVR first  
then scale traffic.

Actual:

Scale traffic first.

CAC deteriora.

No deberíamos aprender:

CRO-first strategy failed.

No se ejecutó.

---

# **26\. Completion Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿Se completó realmente la intervención?

Una intervención puede quedar:

fully completed  
partially completed  
abandoned  
rolled back

---

# **27\. Completion example**

Playbook:

Step 1:  
New welcome offer

Step 2:  
PDP redesign

Step 3:  
Checkout simplification

Step 4:  
Retention flow

Actual:

Steps 1 and 2 completed.  
Step 3 partial.  
Step 4 never launched.

El resultado no evalúa todo el playbook.

---

# **28\. Partial execution**

Debe distinguirse:

PARTIAL EXECUTION

de:

FULL EXECUTION

Una intervención parcial puede ser útil.

Pero la inferencia debe limitarse a lo ejecutado.

---

# **29\. Completion ≠ Success**

Una intervención puede completarse perfectamente y fracasar comercialmente.

execution success  
≠  
business success

Del mismo modo, una ejecución incompleta puede producir un resultado comercial positivo por otros factores.

---

# **30\. Execution Fidelity como vector**

Conceptualmente no debería pensarse únicamente:

HIGH  
LOW

La ejecución puede ser:

scope: high  
parameters: high  
timing: low  
sequence: medium  
completion: high

Esto explica mejor qué parte de la intervención es evaluable.

---

# **31\. No pseudo-precision**

No es necesario afirmar:

Execution Fidelity \= 83.7%

si no existe una base real.

Más útil:

Overall fidelity:  
Moderate.

Main deviation:  
Timing.

Other dimensions:  
High.

---

# **32\. Critical vs Non-critical Deviations**

No toda desviación tiene el mismo peso.

Ejemplo:

Plan:

Launch campaign Monday 09:00.

Actual:

Monday 09:15.

Probablemente irrelevante.

Pero:

Monday  
→  
Friday Black Friday

es crítica.

---

# **33\. Material deviation**

Una desviación es material cuando puede cambiar razonablemente:

mechanism  
exposure  
business effect  
risk  
interpretation

---

# **34\. Cosmetic deviations**

Algunas diferencias pueden no afectar significado.

Ejemplo:

Email scheduled at 10:00  
sent at 10:07.

No todo debe penalizar fidelity.

---

# **35\. Execution Intent**

Debe distinguirse entre:

PLANNED INTENT

y:

ACTUAL ACTION

porque a veces una intervención se modifica conscientemente durante ejecución.

---

# **36\. Adaptive execution**

No todas las modificaciones son errores.

Ejemplo:

Plan:

Increase budget 20%.

Durante ejecución:

inventory demand spikes

Human decides:

cap increase at 10%.

Esto es:

ADAPTIVE EXECUTION

No necesariamente execution failure.

---

# **37\. Adaptation vs Deviation**

## **Deviation**

Cambio no previsto que reduce fidelidad.

## **Adaptation**

Cambio deliberado porque el contexto cambió.

Ambos deben registrarse conceptualmente.

Pero deben interpretarse distinto.

---

# **38\. Adaptive strategy becomes a different experience**

Si la intervención cambia significativamente durante ejecución:

ORIGINAL PLAN  
→  
ADAPTED PLAN

entonces la experiencia debe interpretarse como:

adaptive intervention

no como ejecución pura del plan inicial.

---

# **39\. Human Modification**

Ejemplo:

AI recomienda:

reduce Meta spend 30%.

Manager decide:

reduce 10%.

Outcome:

CAC improves slightly.

No sabemos qué habría ocurrido con \-30%.

La experiencia evalúa:

human-modified intervention

---

# **40\. Override and fidelity**

Cuando existe override:

RECOMMENDED ACTION  
≠  
EXECUTED ACTION

fidelity respecto a recomendación puede ser baja.

Pero execution quality respecto a la decisión humana final puede ser alta.

Esto debe distinguirse.

---

# **41\. Reference point for fidelity**

Siempre debe aclararse:

FIDELITY TO WHAT?

Puede ser:

recommendation  
approved plan  
playbook  
human-modified plan  
final execution plan

Para aprendizaje sobre recomendación, importa fidelidad respecto a la recomendación aprobada.

---

# **42\. Approved Plan vs Original Recommendation**

Ejemplo:

AI recommends:

price \+10%

Human approves:

price \+5%

Execution:

price \+5%

Execution fidelity respecto al plan aprobado:

HIGH

Fidelity respecto a recomendación original:

PARTIAL

Resultado solo evalúa plenamente el plan aprobado.

---

# **43\. Execution quality can include competence**

Más allá de fidelity, existe:

EXECUTION QUALITY

Dos equipos pueden ejecutar el mismo plan con distinta calidad.

Ejemplo:

Landing page redesigned

Plan cumplido.

Pero implementación:

slow  
buggy  
mobile layout broken

Formalmente se completó.

Realmente la calidad es pobre.

---

# **44\. Fidelity vs Execution Quality**

Debe distinguirse:

FIDELITY  
\=  
did we execute the intended intervention?

EXECUTION QUALITY  
\=  
how well was it implemented?

Ambos afectan aprendizaje.

---

# **45\. Example**

Plan:

Improve mobile checkout UX.

Actual:

all planned changes implemented.

Fidelity:

HIGH

Pero:

new JS bug causes occasional payment failure.

Execution quality:

LOW

Outcome negativo no debería atribuirse al concepto de simplificación de checkout.

---

# **46\. Functional correctness**

Execution quality incluye:

Did it work technically?

Ejemplos:

tracking fired  
coupon applied  
campaign served  
checkout loaded  
email rendered  
audience synced

---

# **47\. Semantic correctness**

También:

Did it represent the intended business logic?

Ejemplo:

Campaña técnicamente funciona.

Pero audiencia seleccionada:

wrong customers

Technical execution:

successful

Business execution:

incorrect

---

# **48\. Execution can be technically successful but strategically invalid**

Ejemplo:

Promotion launched successfully.

Pero:

excluded VIP customers  
instead of targeting VIP customers.

Sistema técnico funcionó.

La intervención estratégica no.

---

# **49\. Operational verification**

La IA debe valorar si existe evidencia de que:

the intended action  
actually occurred in the real system.

No basta con:

"Task marked complete."

---

# **50\. Task completion ≠ Execution evidence**

Anti-pattern:

Task status \= done  
→  
intervention executed

Puede haber:

partial rollout  
configuration error  
wrong audience  
failed integration  
silent API error

La ejecución necesita verificación cuando sea material.

---

# **51\. Execution Evidence**

Conceptualmente pueden existir señales de ejecución:

system state changed  
campaign activated  
price updated  
email delivered  
inventory order placed  
checkout variant live

Esto pertenece a Fase 8, pero Fase 9 necesita comprender su importancia.

---

# **52\. Verified Execution vs Reported Execution**

Distinguir:

REPORTED EXECUTION

de:

VERIFIED EXECUTION

Ejemplo:

Manager says:  
campaign launched.

frente a:

platform data confirms:  
campaign active at intended settings.

No siempre se dispondrá de verificación fuerte.

Eso debe influir en confianza.

---

# **53\. Unknown Execution Fidelity**

Puede ocurrir:

We do not know exactly  
what was executed.

Correcto:

execution fidelity unknown

Incorrecto:

assume execution matched plan

---

# **54\. Unknown is not high fidelity**

La ausencia de evidencia de desviación no prueba:

high fidelity

Especialmente en procesos manuales.

---

# **55\. Execution Observability**

Una estrategia que no permite conocer cómo fue ejecutada produce aprendizajes más débiles.

Conceptualmente:

LOW EXECUTION OBSERVABILITY  
→  
LOWER LEARNING CONFIDENCE

---

# **56\. Execution logs are not inherently truth**

Aunque existan logs:

logged action

puede no equivaler a:

business effect actually applied

Ejemplo:

API registra:

discount created

pero tienda no lo muestra por conflicto de reglas.

---

# **57\. Effective Execution**

Concepto importante:

EFFECTIVE EXECUTION

Pregunta:

> ¿La intervención llegó realmente al sistema o usuario que debía experimentarla?

---

# **58\. Delivery Fidelity**

Una intervención puede configurarse correctamente pero no llegar a suficiente población.

Ejemplo:

Email:

flow configured correctly

pero:

deliverability collapsed.

Plan fidelity en configuración:

HIGH

Effective exposure:

LOW

---

# **59\. Exposure**

Necesitamos distinguir:

INTERVENTION CONFIGURED

de:

INTERVENTION EXPERIENCED

Ejemplo:

PDP test está live.

Pero solo:

3% of intended traffic

lo ve por un routing issue.

---

# **60\. Exposure Fidelity**

Pregunta:

> ¿La intervención alcanzó realmente a la población y volumen previstos?

Puede considerarse parte del scope/completion.

Pero conceptualmente merece atención específica.

---

# **61\. Dose**

En algunas estrategias, la intensidad de exposición importa.

Ejemplos:

number of email sends  
discount depth  
ad frequency  
number of sessions exposed  
number of lifecycle touchpoints

La “dosis” real puede diferir de la prevista.

---

# **62\. Dose-response relationship**

Si outcome depende de intensidad, necesitamos saber:

what dose was actually delivered

No basta con:

intervention happened.

---

# **63\. Example — Email**

Plan:

3 emails over 7 days.

Actual:

1 email delivered.  
2 suppressed due to deliverability rules.

No deberíamos aprender:

3-email flow ineffective.

---

# **64\. Example — Paid Media**

Plan:

Scale spend from €1,000/day  
to €1,200/day.

Actual:

algorithm overspends to €1,750/day.

If CAC deteriorates:

planned scaling strategy  
was not faithfully evaluated.

---

# **65\. Example — CRO**

Plan:

Remove mandatory account creation.

Actual:

account creation removed on desktop  
but remained on mobile.

Overall CVR unchanged.

Segment analysis may reveal:

desktop improved  
mobile unchanged

Global outcome would obscure fidelity issue.

---

# **66\. Example — Pricing**

Plan:

Increase price on premium range only.

Actual:

ERP sync updates entire catalog.

Outcome cannot be interpreted as premium-range price experiment.

---

# **67\. Example — Promotion**

Plan:

15% discount to first-time buyers.

Actual:

20% discount  
sitewide  
for 72 hours longer than planned.

This is materially different.

---

# **68\. Example — Inventory**

Plan:

Increase reorder quantity 25%.

Actual:

supplier only fulfilled half.

If stockouts continue, strategy may not have been fully tested.

---

# **69\. Example — Fulfilment**

Plan:

Promise 24h dispatch.

Actual operational capacity:

only 60% shipped in 24h.

If complaints rise:

promise strategy  
and execution capacity  
must be separated.

---

# **70\. Example — Subscription**

Plan:

Offer 20% first-order discount.

Actual:

billing bug also discounts second renewal.

LTV deterioration could stem from implementation defect, not acquisition strategy.

---

# **71\. Example — Retention**

Plan:

post-purchase education flow  
for new buyers.

Actual:

flow activates after every purchase  
including loyal customers.

The audience logic changed.

---

# **72\. Execution Fidelity in Playbooks**

Playbooks are particularly vulnerable because they contain:

multiple steps  
dependencies  
timing  
sequence  
decision branches

Learning from playbooks requires knowing:

which steps actually ran

---

# **73\. Playbook-level outcome**

Suppose playbook:

1\. Improve offer  
2\. Build new creative  
3\. Fix landing page  
4\. Scale traffic

Revenue improves.

If all steps executed:

playbook-level evidence

But attribution to individual steps remains uncertain.

---

# **74\. Step-level fidelity**

Maybe:

Step 1:  
high fidelity

Step 2:  
high fidelity

Step 3:  
partial

Step 4:  
not executed

Then learning should respect this structure.

---

# **75\. Sequence deviation in playbooks**

If step 4 happens before step 3:

scale traffic  
before  
fix landing page

the playbook has not been faithfully executed even if all steps eventually occur.

---

# **76\. Dependencies**

Execution Fidelity must consider prerequisites.

Example:

Launch retention automation

requires:

correct customer segmentation

If segmentation is broken, the nominal action executed but intended intervention did not.

---

# **77\. Hidden Execution Dependencies**

Potential dependencies include:

tracking  
inventory accuracy  
audience sync  
catalog data  
pricing sync  
creative approval  
deliverability  
payment systems

A failure in dependency can invalidate intervention.

---

# **78\. Preconditions**

Before an intervention can be considered faithfully executed, relevant preconditions may need to hold.

Example:

Strategy:

scale bestselling SKU

Precondition:

inventory sufficient

If false, strategy cannot be evaluated normally.

---

# **79\. Execution under violated preconditions**

If recommendation assumes:

stock available

and actual:

stock constrained

then failure may reflect:

precondition violation

not strategy quality.

---

# **80\. Execution Fidelity and Context Shift**

Sometimes plan was correct at decision time.

But environment changes before execution.

Example:

price increase approved Monday

Tuesday:

main competitor launches 25% discount.

Wednesday:

price increase executed unchanged.

Technically fidelity high.

Contextual validity may now be low.

---

# **81\. Faithful execution of outdated plan**

Important distinction:

HIGH EXECUTION FIDELITY  
≠  
CURRENTLY APPROPRIATE EXECUTION

The plan can be executed exactly and still be wrong because context changed.

---

# **82\. Execution freshness**

The Learning Engine should distinguish:

Was the plan still appropriate  
when it was executed?

This is separate from fidelity.

---

# **83\. Delayed approvals**

Example:

Recommendation valid Monday.

Human approval arrives:

3 weeks later

Business state has changed.

Outcome should not be interpreted as clean evidence about original decision context.

---

# **84\. Decision-to-execution latency**

High latency can reduce evaluability.

Because:

DECISION CONTEXT  
≠  
EXECUTION CONTEXT

---

# **85\. Execution latency as operational learning**

If recommendations repeatedly become stale due to:

slow approvals

learning may be:

approval latency is operational bottleneck.

This is a Fase 9.20-type insight, but fidelity provides evidence.

---

# **86\. Intervention contamination**

Execution may be faithful, but simultaneous changes can contaminate evaluation.

Example:

new creative executed perfectly

while:

price changed  
offer changed  
landing page changed

Fidelity high.

Isolation low.

These are different concepts.

---

# **87\. Fidelity vs Isolation**

EXECUTION FIDELITY  
\=  
did we execute intended action?

INTERVENTION ISOLATION  
\=  
did other things change too?

A perfectly executed intervention can still have weak causal evidence because isolation is poor.

---

# **88\. Fidelity vs Evidence Quality**

Likewise:

high fidelity

does not imply:

high evidence quality

Sample may be tiny.

Tracking may fail.

Window may be short.

These belong to later blocks.

---

# **89\. Fidelity is necessary but not sufficient**

A useful mental rule:

HIGH FIDELITY  
IS NECESSARY  
FOR STRONG STRATEGY EVALUATION

BUT

NOT SUFFICIENT

We still need:

good measurement  
adequate sample  
causal attribution  
context

---

# **90\. Low fidelity limits what can be learned**

If fidelity is low:

learning about strategy  
must weaken

But another kind of learning may emerge.

Example:

strategy could not be executed reliably

This itself is useful operational knowledge.

---

# **91\. Strategy Learning vs Execution Learning**

A failed intervention can produce:

NO STRATEGY LEARNING

but:

STRONG EXECUTION LEARNING

Example:

API cannot reliably update campaign budget.

We learned about infrastructure.

Not about media strategy.

---

# **92\. Failure decomposition**

When outcome is negative, the IA should consider:

STRATEGY FAILURE?

EXECUTION FAILURE?

MEASUREMENT FAILURE?

CONTEXT SHIFT?

CONSTRAINT FAILURE?

Never jump directly to strategy failure.

---

Conceptually, execution failures can include:

wrong scope

incomplete execution  
technical failure  
human error  
tool failure  
permission failure  
integration failure  
approval delay  
data failure  
rollback

campaign intended for prospects

# **96\. Wrong Timing**

launch after promotion ended.

traffic scaled before tracking fixed.

half the playbook implemented.

# **99\. Technical Failure**

checkout variant crashes on Safari.

---

# **100\. Human Error**

Example:

wrong discount code configured.

# **101\. Tool Failure**

Example:

email automation platform failed to trigger messages.

---

system could not update campaign due to missing access.

Shopify inventory data did not sync to Meta.

creative approved after opportunity window ended.

audience built from stale customer list.

price increase reverted after two hours.

# **352\. Doctrina central del 9.3**

La IA debe interiorizar:

I must never evaluate a recommendation  
only from the final outcome.

First I must determine whether  
the intended intervention was actually executed.

I need to compare planned and actual execution  
across scope,  
parameters,  
timing,  
sequence  
and completion.

I also need to know whether  
the intervention actually reached  
the intended users or systems,  
whether key preconditions held,  
and whether meaningful execution defects occurred.

If execution materially diverged,  
the outcome may teach me about  
the actual intervention,  
execution capability,  
or operational failure,  
but it should not be used  
as strong evidence against  
or in favor of  
the original recommendation.
