# 9.04 — Requisitos conceptuales de Evidence Quality

## Naturaleza

Este documento NO define un schema técnico.

Extrae las responsabilidades semánticas que una futura representación de Evidence Quality debería poder soportar.

---

## 1. Claim evaluado

Toda valoración de evidence quality debe estar vinculada a una afirmación concreta.

Debe poder conocerse:

- claim;
- scope;
- population;
- metric;
- time horizon.

No existe evidencia “alta” en abstracto sin saber para qué claim.

---

## 2. Relevant Sample

Debe poder representarse conceptualmente:

- number of relevant observations;
- exposed population;
- control/reference population when relevant;
- sample composition;
- population of interest;
- rare-event context;
- sample stability.

---

## 3. Representativeness

Debe poder conocerse:

- who is represented;
- who is excluded;
- channel mix;
- customer mix;
- device mix;
- geographic mix;
- whether treatment population matches target population.

---

## 4. Sample Imbalance

Cuando existan grupos comparativos debe poder conservarse:

- relative size;
- exposure imbalance;
- whether imbalance reduces precision or comparability.

---

## 5. Data Quality

Debe poder describirse conceptualmente:

- accuracy;
- completeness;
- consistency;
- freshness;
- duplication;
- definition;
- currency/unit consistency.

---

## 6. Metric Definition

Debe poder preservarse:

- metric name;
- semantic definition;
- denominator;
- inclusions/exclusions;
- version;
- source methodology.

No se define estructura técnica.

---

## 7. Metric Version

Cuando cambie una definición, debe poder distinguirse:

`OLD METRIC DEFINITION`

de:

`NEW METRIC DEFINITION`

para evitar comparaciones inválidas.

---

## 8. Data Source

Debe poder identificarse procedencia conceptual:

- transactional system;
- ad platform;
- analytics;
- ERP;
- CRM;
- warehouse;
- manual spreadsheet;
- human report;
- customer feedback;
- survey;
- reviews.

---

## 9. Source Semantics

La representación debería permitir distinguir qué mide cada fuente.

Ejemplo conceptual:

- transaction system → business event;
- ad platform → attributed delivery/performance;
- analytics → sessions/behavior;
- human source → qualitative context.

---

## 10. Source Reliability

Debe poder expresarse conceptualmente si una fuente es:

- direct;
- indirect;
- verified;
- self-reported;
- qualitative;
- automated;
- manual;
- partially observable.

---

## 11. Missing Data

Debe poder representar:

- what is missing;
- why it is missing;
- whether missingness is known;
- whether missingness may be systematic;
- affected population;
- affected claims.

---

## 12. Unknown vs Zero

La representación debe distinguir explícitamente:

- unknown;
- zero;
- not applicable;
- unavailable;
- partial.

---

## 13. Measurement Quality

Debe poder conocerse:

- what phenomenon is intended to be measured;
- actual metric used;
- whether metric is direct or proxy;
- measurement validity;
- instrumentation state;
- tracking changes.

---

## 14. Measurement Accuracy

Debe poder separarse de validity.

Un dato puede estar técnicamente bien medido y medir el fenómeno equivocado.

---

## 15. Measurement Coverage

Debe poder representar qué parte del negocio está observada:

- online only;
- excluding retail;
- excluding marketplace;
- excluding phone;
- privacy-restricted segments;
- offline effects.

---

## 16. Attribution Method

Cuando sea relevante debe poder conocerse:

- platform attribution;
- attribution window;
- change of attribution method;
- potential double counting.

---

## 17. Tracking State

Debe poder representarse:

- tracking stable?;
- instrumentation changed?;
- known outages?;
- duplicate events?;
- missing UTMs?;
- broken webhooks?;
- server/client differences?

---

## 18. Observation Window

Debe poder conservarse:

- start;
- end;
- duration;
- expected causal timing;
- maturity per metric;
- context drift;
- novelty period;
- stabilization period.

---

## 19. Evidence Maturity

Debe poder expresarse por claim/metric:

- provisional;
- developing;
- mature.

No se prescribe enum técnico obligatorio.

---

## 20. Metric Finality

Debe poder indicarse si un dato:

- final;
- still revising;
- delayed;
- pending returns;
- pending churn;
- pending repeat behavior.

---

## 21. Noise

Debe poder describirse conceptualmente:

- normal variance;
- observed variance;
- volatility;
- daily/weekly seasonality;
- auction volatility;
- operational noise;
- outlier sensitivity.

---

## 22. Outliers

Debe poder distinguirse si un outlier es:

- real business event;
- measurement error;
- unknown;
- material;
- representative or exceptional.

---

## 23. Heterogeneity

La representación debería poder conservar outcomes relevantes por:

- segment;
- channel;
- device;
- geography;
- cohort;
- product;
- lifecycle stage.

---

## 24. Evidence Relevance

Debe poder indicarse qué claim sostiene realmente una evidencia.

Ejemplos:

- supports engagement claim;
- does not support profitability claim;
- supports mobile only;
- does not support all markets.

---

## 25. Direct vs Indirect Evidence

Debe poder distinguirse:

- direct business evidence;
- proxy evidence;
- qualitative evidence;
- leading indicator;
- final outcome.

---

## 26. Evidence Provenance

Debe poder rastrearse conceptualmente:

`BUSINESS EVENT → MEASUREMENT → DATA → METRIC → OBSERVATION → EVIDENCE`

para revisar distorsiones.

---

## 27. Evidence Independence

Cuando varias señales soportan un claim debe poder conocerse si:

- independent;
- derived from same event;
- correlated;
- duplicated.

---

## 28. Triangulation

Debe poder reflejarse si varias fuentes diferentes convergen sobre una misma interpretación.

---

## 29. Evidence Conflict

Cuando fuentes discrepan debe poder conservarse:

- source A;
- source B;
- methodological difference;
- unresolved conflict;
- confidence reduction.

No debe forzarse reconciliación ficticia.

---

## 30. Evidence Direction

Una evidencia puede:

- support;
- weaken;
- not materially change;

una creencia.

---

## 31. Evidence Quality Dimensions

Debe poder expresarse conceptualmente:

- sample adequacy;
- representativeness;
- data accuracy;
- data completeness;
- measurement validity;
- measurement consistency;
- observation maturity;
- noise level;
- missingness;
- source reliability;
- relevance.

---

## 32. Evidence Quality per metric

La misma experiencia puede tener distinta calidad por métrica.

No debe forzarse un único estado global.

---

## 33. Evidence Quality per claim

La misma evidencia puede ser fuerte para una afirmación y débil para otra.

---

## 34. Evidence Strength

Debe poder expresarse conceptualmente con categorías como:

- low;
- medium;
- high;

acompañadas de explicación.

La fuente no exige score numérico.

---

## 35. Strengths y Limitations

La evaluación debería poder explicar:

- main strengths;
- main limitations;
- dominant weakness;
- unresolved uncertainties.

---

## 36. Evidence Sufficiency

Debe poder distinguirse:

- absolute quality;
- sufficiency for a specific decision.

La misma evidencia puede ser suficiente para experimentar e insuficiente para rollout global.

---

## 37. Decision Context

Cuando se evalúa sufficiency, debe poder considerarse:

- action consequence;
- reversibility;
- business risk;
- urgency;
- downside asymmetry.

---

## 38. Evidence Gap

Debe poder expresarse conceptualmente cuando:

`DECISION IMPORTANCE > EVIDENCE STRENGTH`

---

## 39. Evidence Lifecycle

Evidence Quality debe poder revisarse:

- provisional;
- developing;
- mature;
- downgraded;
- invalidated.

---

## 40. Invalid Evidence

Debe poder marcarse cuando el fundamento esté roto por:

- corrupted data;
- wrong cohort;
- broken denominator;
- duplicate tracking;
- wrong date range.

Invalid evidence no debe acumularse como soporte normal.

---

## 41. Discovery vs Confirmation

Debe poder distinguirse si un pattern fue:

- ex ante hypothesis;
- post hoc discovery;
- confirmation attempt;
- replication.

---

## 42. Robustness

Debe poder conservarse si el finding:

- survives reasonable period changes;
- survives segment changes;
- depends on one outlier;
- is highly fragile.

---

## 43. Reassessment

Una futura representación debería permitir revisar evidence quality cuando aparezca nueva información.

---

## 44. Evidence Quality Statement

Sin imponer schema, una evaluación debería poder responder:

- Sample adequacy: [...]
- Representativeness: [...]
- Data quality: [...]
- Measurement quality: [...]
- Observation maturity: [...]
- Noise: [...]
- Missing information: [...]
- Overall evidence strength: [...]
- Main limitations: [...]

---

## 45. Lineage

Debe poder mantenerse la cadena:

`OBSERVATION`
→ `EVIDENCE QUALITY`
→ `ATTRIBUTION`
→ `LEARNING`

Evidence Quality no sustituye las fases posteriores.
