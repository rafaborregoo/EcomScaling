# FASE 7.13 — Stop / Continue / Scale / Rollback

## Propósito

Una recomendación no termina cuando se inicia una intervención.

El Motor debe poder responder:

> ¿Qué hacemos ahora que la intervención ya está en marcha?

Las decisiones principales son:

```text
STOP
CONTINUE
SCALE
ROLLBACK
```

Principio central:

```text
Starting an Intervention
≠
Committing to It Forever
```

La recomendación inicial es una hipótesis operativa que debe actualizarse con realidad observada.

---

## 1. Intervención como hipótesis operativa

Incluso con buena evidencia previa:

```text
Recommendation
→ Intervention
→ Observed Reality
```

La realidad puede:

- confirmar;
- contradecir;
- matizar;
- invalidar;

la expectativa inicial.

Por tanto:

```text
Initial Recommendation
≠
Permanent Truth
```

---

## 2. Las cuatro decisiones fundamentales

### Stop

Detener el compromiso futuro de la intervención.

### Continue

Mantener la exposición aproximadamente en su nivel actual mientras se sigue observando.

### Scale

Aumentar el compromiso:

- magnitude;
- scope;
- velocity;
- duration;
- investment.

### Rollback

Intentar regresar al estado anterior.

---

## 3. Stop ≠ Rollback

```text
Stop
=
Do not continue forward
```

```text
Rollback
=
Reverse what has already changed
```

Una campaña puede detenerse sin necesidad de revertir nada.

Un pricing change puede requerir restaurar el estado previo.

---

## 4. Continue ≠ Do Nothing

Continuar significa:

```text
Maintain Current Intervention
```

Puede requerir:

- monitoring;
- maintenance;
- operation;
- observation.

Es una decisión activa de:

```text
Do Not Change Exposure Yet
```

---

## 5. Scale ≠ Continue

Scale significa:

```text
Increase Commitment
```

No simplemente mantener lo que ya funciona.

---

## 6. Scale is a new decision

Error:

```text
Intervention Works
→ Automatically Scale
```

Pregunta correcta:

> ¿La siguiente unidad de exposición sigue creando valor?

---

## 7. Average Success ≠ Marginal Success

```text
Performance at Current Scale
≠
Performance at Larger Scale
```

Una intervención puede ser rentable a pequeña escala y perder atractivo al expandirse.

---

## 8. Intervention Lifecycle

Conceptualmente:

```text
Start
↓
Observe
↓
Stop / Continue / Scale / Rollback
↓
Observe Again
↓
New Decision
```

La lógica es iterativa.

---

## 9. Commitment Bias y Sunk Cost

Anti-pattern:

```text
We started it
→ We continue it
```

El coste pasado no debe controlar la decisión futura.

Pregunta:

```text
From Now Forward,
is continuing still worthwhile?
```

Conceptualmente:

```text
Continue
if
Expected Remaining Value
>
Expected Remaining Cost + Risk
```

No como fórmula rígida.

---

## 10. Stop Decision

Stop es apropiado cuando la intervención deja de justificar future commitment.

Puede ocurrir porque:

- no funciona;
- economics son pobres;
- risk aumenta;
- context cambia;
- constraint aparece;
- opportunity cost aumenta;
- evidence invalida la hipótesis.

---

## 11. Positive ≠ Worth Continuing

Una intervención ligeramente positiva puede merecer Stop si existe una mejor alternativa para el mismo recurso escaso.

---

## 12. Stop because mechanism failed

Si la intervención no mueve el mecanismo esperado y la evidencia es suficiente:

```text
STOP
```

---

## 13. Stop because economics failed

```text
Revenue ↑
but
Contribution ↓↓↓
```

no es éxito económico.

---

## 14. Stop because risk / context changed

Una acción correcta ayer puede ser incorrecta hoy si cambian:

- supplier;
- stock;
- cash;
- platform;
- market;
- regulations;
- operations.

---

## 15. Stop and Learn

Detener no significa olvidar.

```text
Stop Intervention
≠
Discard Learning
```

Un fracaso comercial puede producir decision value.

---

## 16. Continue Decision

Continue significa que:

```text
Current Exposure
remains justified
```

pero no existe evidencia suficiente para aumentar commitment.

---

## 17. Continue under positive but immature evidence

Ejemplo:

- early positive signal;
- small sample;
- few cycles;
- high variability.

Entonces:

```text
CONTINUE
```

puede ser mejor que Scale.

---

## 18. Continue at Current Effective Scale

Una intervención puede estar en su rango óptimo actual.

Si más exposición introduce diminishing returns:

```text
CONTINUE
```

puede ser correcto.

---

## 19. Continue and Observe

Útil cuando faltan outcomes tardíos:

- repeat;
- LTV;
- returns;
- refund rate;
- cohort quality.

---

## 20. Stabilization Window

Después de cambios importantes puede ser necesario:

```text
Allow System to Stabilize
```

antes de añadir otra modificación.

---

## 21. Scale Decision

Scale significa aumentar exposure porque existe evidence suficiente de que:

```text
More Commitment
has positive expected value
```

---

## 22. Repeatability before Scale

Modelo mental:

```text
Signal
→ Validation
→ Repeatability
→ Scale
```

No siempre como secuencia formal.

---

## 23. Evidence should rise with Commitment

```text
More Scale
→ More Evidence Required
```

especialmente si aumentan:

- downside;
- irreversibility;
- blast radius.

---

## 24. Progressive Scaling

Scale no tiene que ser:

```text
1x → 10x
```

Puede ser:

```text
1x → 1.5x → 2x → Observe
```

conceptualmente.

---

## 25. Scale has four dimensions

Puede aumentar:

```text
Magnitude
Scope
Velocity
Duration
```

No todas deben crecer a la vez.

---

## 26. Scale Readiness

Intervention Success no implica Scale Readiness.

Antes de ampliar:

- inventory;
- operations;
- support;
- cash;
- creative pipeline;
- process maturity;
- technical capacity.

---

## 27. Success can invalidate further scaling

Una intervención puede funcionar tan bien que cree un nuevo constraint.

Ejemplo:

```text
Paid works
→ Stock nearly depleted
→ Continue / Reduce
not Scale
```

---

## 28. Marginal Economics

La pregunta no es:

```text
Is average CAC good?
```

sino:

```text
What happens at the next spend level?
```

---

## 29. Scale until economics or constraints change

Conceptualmente:

```text
Scale
↓
Observe Marginal Economics
↓
Observe Constraints
↓
Continue / Stop Scaling
```

---

## 30. Scale ≠ Maximum Scale

El objetivo no es Maximum Volume.

Es:

```text
Economically Rational Scale
```

---

## 31. Stop Scaling ≠ Stop Intervention

Clave:

```text
Stop Scaling
≠
Stop Intervention
```

Una campaña puede seguir siendo rentable a su nivel actual aunque ya no tenga headroom atractivo.

---

## 32. Plateau ≠ Failure

Un plateau puede indicar:

```text
Current Effective Scale
```

Si economics siguen sanos:

```text
CONTINUE
```

puede ser correcto.

---

## 33. De-scale

Puede existir:

```text
Reduce Exposure
```

sin llevar future commitment a cero.

```text
De-scale
=
smaller commitment
```

```text
Stop
=
zero future commitment
```

---

## 34. Adaptive Intensity

```text
Intervention Intensity
should respond
to evidence
```

Puede:

- increase;
- hold;
- decrease;
- stop.

---

## 35. Rollback Decision

Rollback es apropiado cuando:

```text
Current State
is materially worse
than Previous State
```

y la reversión es razonablemente valiosa y viable.

---

## 36. Rollback requires baseline awareness

Debe conocerse:

```text
Previous State
```

Sin baseline claro, rollback puede ser ambiguo.

---

## 37. Rollback vs Fix Forward

No siempre debe regresarse al estado anterior.

### Rollback

Volver al previous state.

### Fix Forward

Mantener el nuevo state y corregir defects.

---

## 38. Rollback when previous state is safer

Más razonable cuando:

- damage clear;
- recent change;
- easy reversal;
- previous state known.

---

## 39. Fix Forward when rollback is costly

Especialmente cuando revertir puede crear:

- downtime;
- inconsistency;
- data errors;
- more operational risk.

---

## 40. Stop ≠ Rollback ≠ Fix Forward

```text
Stop
= no further commitment

Rollback
= return to previous state

Fix Forward
= stay in new state and repair
```

---

## 41. Transitional Performance

Algunas intervenciones producen:

```text
Short-Term Disruption
```

antes de generar valor.

Ejemplos:

- replatforming;
- process redesign;
- pricing transitions.

Por tanto:

```text
Temporary Dip
≠
Structural Failure
```

---

## 42. Don't rationalize all failure as temporary

El extremo opuesto también es peligroso.

Debe existir causal basis para justificar una adaptation period.

---

## 43. Evaluation must be System-Aware

Observar al menos:

- intended mechanism;
- primary outcome;
- secondary effects;
- economics;
- risk;
- constraints;
- quality.

---

## 44. Target Metric Improved ≠ Intervention Succeeded

Ejemplo:

```text
Email Revenue ↑
but
Unsubscribe ↑↑
Customer Fatigue ↑
```

puede ser mala intervención.

---

## 45. Guardrail breach

Una intervención puede mejorar su objetivo principal y aun así no merecer Scale.

Puede justificar:

- hold;
- de-scale;
- stop;
- rollback.

---

## 46. Partial Decisions

No todo es all-or-nothing.

Puede existir:

```text
Stop Segment B
Continue Segment A
```

```text
Scale SKU A
Hold SKU B
```

```text
Rollback Component X
Keep Component Y
```

---

## 47. Do not globalize local failure

Intervention Effect puede variar por:

- segment;
- channel;
- market;
- SKU;
- cohort.

---

## 48. Leading vs Lagging Evidence

### Leading

- CTR;
- engagement;
- add-to-cart.

### Lagging

- repeat;
- LTV;
- return rate;
- contribution.

No escalar exclusivamente por leading signal.

---

## 49. Evaluation Window

Debe ser suficiente para observar el mechanism y material outcomes.

No existe duración universal.

---

## 50. Asymmetric Evidence Requirements

Conceptualmente:

```text
Potential Harm High
→ Lower Evidence Needed to Stop
```

```text
Commitment Increase High
→ Higher Evidence Needed to Scale
```

---

## 51. Continue under Ambiguity

Si evidence es mixed y downside controlado:

```text
CONTINUE
+
OBSERVE
```

puede ser correcto.

---

## 52. Sufficient Confidence, not Perfect Certainty

No esperar certeza absoluta.

La pregunta es:

> ¿Existe suficiente confidence para este tamaño de commitment?

---

## 53. Proportionality

Conceptualmente:

```text
Evidence Required
∝
Commitment Size
+
Downside
+
Irreversibility
```

No fórmula técnica.

---

## 54. Intervention Health

Puede razonarse conceptualmente como:

- healthy;
- uncertain;
- underperforming;
- harmful.

No enums técnicos.

---

## 55. Failure vs Implementation Failure

Diferenciar:

```text
Wrong Idea
```

de:

```text
Bad Execution
```

Antes de concluir:

> ¿La intervención se implementó realmente como estaba diseñada?

---

## 56. Intervention Fidelity

Poor execution puede invalidar inferencia sobre strategy.

```text
Poor Execution
→
Cannot confidently judge strategy
```

---

## 57. Adjust before Stop

No toda intervention que falla debe descartarse.

Puede existir:

```text
Correct Mechanism
+
Poor Execution
```

Opciones:

- fix execution;
- tune parameters;
- retry;
- reduce scope.

---

## 58. Continue with Adjustment

Puede existir:

```text
CONTINUE
+
MODIFY PARAMETERS
```

cambiando:

- scope;
- magnitude;
- audience;
- timing;
- offer.

---

## 59. Conditional Scale

Puede existir:

```text
Scale
provided that
Guardrails remain intact
```

No con thresholds universales.

---

## 60. Pre-mortem before Scale

Pregunta:

> ¿Qué es más probable que se rompa primero si escalamos?

---

## 61. Scale Readiness can be the new bottleneck

El éxito de una intervención puede revelar que ahora el limitador es:

- inventory;
- creative capacity;
- cash;
- operations;
- process maturity.

---

## 62. Portfolio-Aware Decisions

Scale one intervention puede requerir:

```text
Reduce / Stop Another
```

Stop puede liberar:

- cash;
- people;
- attention.

Scale consume recursos.

Por tanto:

```text
Stop / Continue / Scale / Rollback
=
Resource Allocation Decisions
under New Evidence
```

---

## 63. Rollback has Cost

Revertir puede requerir:

- development;
- communication;
- operations;
- customer re-education.

No asumir que rollback es gratis.

---

## 64. Technical Reversibility ≠ Business Reversibility

Puede existir:

- technical reversibility;
- commercial reversibility;
- operational reversibility;
- brand reversibility.

Un cambio técnicamente reversible puede dejar residual damage.

---

## 65. Cost of Rollback

Debe considerarse:

```text
Cost to Undo
+
Residual Damage
```

---

## 66. Early Rollback vs Lost Learning

Con clear harm:

> early rollback puede ser más barato.

Con weak evidence:

> premature rollback puede destruir learning.

---

## 67. Emergency vs Strategic Rollback

### Emergency

Critical system failure.

Puede requerir respuesta rápida.

### Strategic

Deterioro sostenido de economics.

Requiere más deliberación.

---

## 68. Precommitment

Antes de ejecutar conviene tener claridad conceptual sobre:

- what would make us stop;
- what would justify scale;
- what would justify continue;
- what would justify rollback.

Esto reduce:

- sunk-cost bias;
- panic;
- post-hoc rationalization.

---

## 69. Decision Map

Conceptualmente:

```text
Positive + Scalable
→ Scale

Positive + Uncertain
→ Continue

Neutral + Informative
→ Continue / Adjust

Negative + Reversible
→ Stop / Rollback

Negative + Irreversible
→ Stop / Fix Forward
```

No algoritmo rígido.

---

## 70. Trend vs Level

Una intervention puede estar:

```text
Below Target
but Improving
```

o:

```text
Above Target
but Deteriorating
```

El nivel actual no basta.

---

## 71. Baseline Quality

Before/After comparison puede engañar si baseline era:

- anomalous;
- seasonal;
- promotional;
- context-shifted.

---

## 72. Attribution and Incrementality

Observed Improvement no implica necesariamente que la intervención lo causó.

Debe diferenciar:

```text
Observed Lift
```

de:

```text
Confidence that intervention caused it
```

y:

```text
Attributed Value
```

de:

```text
Incremental Value
```

---

## 73. Cannibalization and Pull-forward

Scale puede ser incorrecto si el uplift proviene de:

- demand shifting;
- channel cannibalization;
- future sales pulled forward.

---

## 74. Outcome Quality

Antes de Scale:

> ¿Estamos generando más customers valiosos o solo más volume?

Considerar:

- refund rate;
- retention;
- support load;
- margin;
- customer quality.

---

## 75. Delayed Downside

Algunas acciones muestran:

```text
Immediate Upside
+
Delayed Downside
```

No escalar antes de observar second-order effects cuando sean materialmente relevantes.

---

## 76. Reflexive Interventions

Una intervención puede cambiar las condiciones que la hacían rentable.

Ejemplo:

```text
Scale Channel
→ Audience Saturation
→ Performance ↓
```

---

## 77. Path Dependence

Scale outcome puede depender de cómo se escaló:

```text
Gradual
vs
Aggressive
```

---

## 78. Controlled Scaling

```text
Increase
→ Observe
→ Increase
```

permite adaptación.

---

## 79. Tactic Failure ≠ Goal Failure

Detener una táctica no significa abandonar el objetivo.

```text
Stop Tactic
≠
Abandon Goal
```

Puede cambiarse de mechanism.

---

## 80. Outcome over Tactic Attachment

La IA debe estar comprometida con:

```text
Outcome
```

no con:

```text
Specific Tactic
```

---

## 81. Maturity changes thresholds, not logic

### Early

- smaller increments;
- more learning.

### Scaling

- marginal economics;
- capacity.

### Mature

- reallocation;
- incrementality;
- system effects.

---

## 82. Constraint-Aware Scale

Una intervención puede merecer Scale en teoría pero no en práctica.

```text
Scale only to feasible frontier
```

---

## 83. Mixed Outcomes

No todo resultado es success/failure.

Puede existir:

```text
Revenue ↑
Margin ↓
Retention ↑
```

Requiere dominant business objective y trade-off reasoning.

---

## 84. Sticky Commitments

Escalar mediante:

- fixed hires;
- inventory commitments;
- long contracts;

requiere más evidence que una exposure fácilmente reversible.

---

## 85. Variable vs Fixed Commitment

Variable commitment puede preservar optionality.

Pero no es siempre económicamente superior.

---

## 86. Mitigation

Si rollback no es plenamente posible:

- reduce exposure;
- contain damage;
- adjust surrounding system.

---

## 87. Heroics Do Not Scale

Si success depende de:

```text
one person working heroically
```

no es repeatable scale.

---

## 88. Good Enough State

Una intervención puede llegar a:

```text
Good Enough
```

donde:

```text
Further Optimization
has low marginal value
```

Entonces:

- stop optimizing;
- continue operating.

---

## 89. Project State vs Operational State

Una intervención puede salir del:

```text
Active Improvement Portfolio
```

pero permanecer como:

```text
Business As Usual
```

---

## 90. Graduation

Una iniciativa puede terminar en:

```text
Standardized
```

no solo en Stop.

---

## 91. Temporary Interventions Need Exit

Promotions y otros temporary interventions deben tener:

```text
Expected End Condition
```

para evitar permanentization by inertia.

---

## 92. Decision Rationale Must Be Causal

Cada decisión debe responder:

> ¿Qué cambió en nuestra creencia sobre la intervención?

- Stop: belief in current intervention weakens.
- Continue: belief remains unresolved or sufficient at current level.
- Scale: evidence supports greater exposure.
- Rollback: new state is sufficiently worse than previous alternative.

---

## 93. Decision Asymmetry

Errores distintos tienen costes distintos.

### False Stop

Missed upside.

### False Continue

Ongoing waste.

### False Scale

Magnified downside.

### False Rollback

Lost improvement + transition cost.

Por tanto:

> Cost of Error should shape decision threshold.

---

## 94. Recommendation Confidence ≠ Scale Confidence

Puede existir alta confidence en que una acción es útil y baja confidence en que deba ampliarse mucho.

---

## 95. Headroom

Pregunta:

> ¿Cuánto upside adicional queda?

Low headroom reduce atractivo de Scale.

High headroom lo aumenta.

---

## 96. Different Scaling Curves

No todas las intervenciones escalan igual.

Puede haber:

- fixed-cost leverage;
- variable-cost scaling;
- capacity-limited scaling;
- diseconomies of coordination.

---

## 97. Intervention Type Matters

Conceptualmente:

- experiment;
- optimization;
- fix;
- structural change;
- scaling action.

La semántica de Stop/Continue/Scale/Rollback cambia según tipo.

---

## 98. Experiment Outcomes

Puede existir:

- Win;
- Loss;
- Inconclusive.

### Win

Consider Scale.

### Loss

Consider Stop / Rollback.

### Inconclusive

Continue / Redesign / Gather Evidence.

Inconclusive es válido, pero no eternamente.

---

## 99. Information Economics

Seguir aprendiendo consume recursos.

Si conceptualmente:

```text
Expected Value of More Information
<
Cost of Learning
```

debe tomarse una decisión.

---

## 100. Regime Change

Cuando el entorno cambia materialmente:

```text
Past Performance
may no longer predict
Future Performance
```

Puede requerir nueva evidence.

---

## 101. Seasonal Scale

Scale puede ser temporal.

Ejemplo:

```text
Black Friday
→ Scale
Post-Black Friday
→ Normalize
```

Normalize no es necesariamente Rollback.

---

## 102. Unsustainable Success

Puede existir:

```text
Business Outcome Positive
+
Execution Model Unsustainable
```

No debe escalarse hasta construir capacity.

---

## 103. Measurement Confidence

Si result depende de data dudosa:

```text
Do Not Scale Aggressively
```

Data uncertainty puede favorecer Continue.

Data corruption puede requerir Pause.

---

## 104. Decision Communication

La salida debe decir primero:

```text
Decision:
STOP / CONTINUE / SCALE / ROLLBACK
```

y después explicar:

- why;
- why not alternatives;
- scope;
- limits;
- what to observe;
- what evidence changes the decision.

---

## 105. Fake Precision Prohibited

No inventar:

```text
Scale if ROAS > 3
```

sin economics/context.

Thresholds deben ser contextuales.

---

## 106. Core Decision Model

La IA puede pensar:

1. What was the intervention supposed to achieve?
2. Did the expected mechanism activate?
3. What actually happened?
4. Is the outcome incremental?
5. What happened to economics?
6. What happened to guardrails?
7. Did new constraints appear?
8. Is the result repeatable?
9. Is there remaining headroom?
10. What is marginal value of more exposure?
11. What is downside of continuing?
12. What is cost of stopping?
13. What is cost of rollback?
14. What alternative use exists for resources?
15. Stop, Continue, Scale or Rollback?

---

## 107. Decision Ordering

Conceptualmente:

```text
Material Harm?
├─ Yes → Stop / Rollback / Fix Forward
└─ No
   ├─ Positive Value?
   │  ├─ No → Stop / Adjust
   │  └─ Yes
   │     ├─ Enough Evidence + Headroom? → Scale
   │     └─ Not Yet → Continue
```

No algoritmo rígido.

---

## 108. Stop Principle

> Stop when expected future value no longer justifies future exposure.

---

## 109. Continue Principle

> Continue when current exposure remains justified but additional commitment is not yet justified.

---

## 110. Scale Principle

> Scale when incremental exposure has attractive expected value and the system can absorb it.

---

## 111. Rollback Principle

> Rollback when the new state is materially worse and returning to the previous state creates more value than fixing forward.

---

## 112. Progressive Evidence Principle

```text
Small Commitment
requires
less evidence

Large Commitment
requires
more evidence
```

---

## 113. Marginal Scaling Principle

```text
Historical Success
≠
Unlimited Future Scale
```

---

## 114. System Principle

```text
Local KPI Success
≠
System Success
```

---

## 115. Opportunity Cost Principle

A positive intervention can still deserve Stop if resources have a better use.

---

## 116. Reversibility Principle

```text
Lower Reversibility
→ Higher Evidence Requirement
```

---

## 117. Learning Principle

```text
Failed Intervention
can still create
valuable learning
```

---

## 118. Stability Principle

Do not change a functioning system without sufficient reason.

---

## 119. Adaptation Principle

Do not continue a failing system because it was once recommended.

---

## 120. Scale Readiness Principle

```text
Intervention Success
≠
Scale Readiness
```

---

## 121. Final Principle

Una IA experta no se mide solo por acertar la primera recomendación.

También por saber:

- cuándo insistir;
- cuándo mantener;
- cuándo ampliar;
- cuándo reducir;
- cuándo detener;
- cuándo revertir;
- cuándo corregir hacia delante.

La regla más importante:

```text
Do not defend
the previous recommendation.

Defend
the best current decision.
```
