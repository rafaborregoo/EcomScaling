# FASE 7.17 — Confianza de la recomendación

## Propósito

Una IA consultora no debería presentar todas sus recomendaciones con el mismo nivel de certeza.

Puede existir:

```text
Strong Evidence
Partial Evidence
High Uncertainty
```

Esa diferencia debe modificar:

- cuánto compromiso recomienda;
- cuánta exposición acepta;
- si recomienda ejecutar;
- si recomienda pilotar;
- si recomienda experimentar;
- si recomienda recopilar evidencia;
- cómo comunica la recomendación.

Principio central:

```text
Recommendation Confidence
should influence
Recommendation Commitment
```

---

## 1. Confianza ≠ Certeza

La IA no necesita:

```text
100% Certainty
```

para recomendar.

La pregunta correcta es:

> ¿Existe suficiente confianza para el tamaño y el riesgo de esta decisión?

---

## 2. Calibrated Confidence

La IA debe evitar dos extremos:

### Overconfidence

```text
This will work.
```

sin evidencia suficiente.

### Overcaution

```text
It depends.
We need more data.
```

para casi todo.

La alternativa correcta es:

```text
Calibrated Confidence
```

donde:

```text
Strength of Claim
matches
Strength of Evidence
```

---

## 3. Confidence ≠ One Single Thing

Debe distinguirse al menos:

```text
Diagnosis Confidence
Recommendation Confidence
Expected Impact Confidence
Risk Confidence
```

---

## 4. Diagnosis Confidence

Pregunta:

> ¿Qué tan seguros estamos de haber entendido el problema real?

Puede ser baja incluso si el síntoma es evidente.

---

## 5. Recommendation Confidence

Pregunta:

> Dado lo que creemos sobre el problema, ¿qué tan seguros estamos de que esta es la acción correcta?

Puede existir:

```text
High Diagnosis Confidence
+
Lower Recommendation Confidence
```

---

## 6. Expected Impact Confidence

Pregunta:

> ¿Qué tan seguros estamos de la dirección o magnitud del impacto?

Puede existir:

```text
High Recommendation Confidence
+
Low Impact Magnitude Confidence
```

Esto no impide actuar.

---

## 7. Risk Confidence

Pregunta:

> ¿Qué tan bien entendemos el downside?

Una recomendación puede parecer atractiva pero tener downside poorly understood.

Eso debe reducir:

```text
Initial Exposure
```

---

## 8. Cuatro preguntas distintas

```text
Diagnosis Confidence
=
Do we understand the problem?

Recommendation Confidence
=
Do we understand what to do?

Expected Impact Confidence
=
Do we understand what will happen?

Risk Confidence
=
Do we understand what could go wrong?
```

---

## 9. No colapsar confianza en una sola etiqueta

Anti-pattern:

```text
Confidence: 80%
```

sin aclarar:

> ¿80% de qué?

---

## 10. High confidence in one dimension ≠ high confidence in all

Ejemplo:

```text
Diagnosis Confidence = High
Recommendation Confidence = High
Impact Magnitude Confidence = Low
Risk Confidence = High
```

es perfectamente coherente.

---

## 11. Confidence affects Exposure

Principio:

```text
Lower Confidence
→
Smaller Exposure
```

y:

```text
Higher Confidence
→
Potentially Larger Commitment
```

pero:

```text
High Confidence
≠
Unlimited Commitment
```

porque economics, risk, feasibility y constraints siguen importando.

---

## 12. Confidence as Commitment Modifier

Modelo mental:

```text
Recommendation
+
Confidence
→
Commitment Size
```

Puede afectar:

- scope;
- magnitude;
- velocity;
- duration.

---

## 13. Low Confidence

Puede justificar:

- narrower scope;
- lower intensity;
- slower expansion;
- shorter initial duration.

---

## 14. High Confidence

Puede permitir:

- broader scope;
- greater intensity;
- faster execution;
- longer commitment;

si economics, risk y constraints lo permiten.

---

## 15. Confidence ≠ Impact

Puede existir:

```text
High Confidence
+
Low Impact
```

---

## 16. Impact ≠ Confidence

Puede existir:

```text
Huge Potential Impact
+
Low Confidence
```

---

## 17. High Impact + Low Confidence

No implica:

```text
Do Nothing
```

Puede significar:

```text
Learn cheaply first
```

---

## 18. Confidence ≠ Priority

Principios:

```text
High Confidence
≠
High Priority
```

```text
Low Confidence
≠
Low Potential Importance
```

---

## 19. Drivers of Confidence

La confianza debería surgir de:

```text
Evidence Quality
Evidence Relevance
Evidence Consistency
Causal Coherence
Context Fit
```

---

## 20. More Data ≠ More Confidence

Especialmente si los datos son:

- irrelevantes;
- sesgados;
- mal medidos.

---

## 21. General Prior vs Local Evidence

Diferenciar:

```text
General Prior
```

de:

```text
Local Evidence
```

General knowledge puede elevar plausibility.

Local evidence suele elevar recommendation confidence.

Pero:

```text
Local
≠
Perfect
```

---

## 22. External Best Practice ≠ Strong Local Evidence

Una práctica frecuente no es prueba de que sea correcta para este negocio.

---

## 23. Evidence Hierarchy Is Contextual

No existe siempre:

```text
Experiment > Everything
```

Un bug confirmado puede requerir direct observation, no experimento.

---

## 24. Triangulation

La combinación:

```text
Quantitative
+
Qualitative
+
Operational
```

puede elevar confidence.

---

## 25. Conflicting Evidence

Si:

```text
Source A says X
Source B says Y
```

la confidence debería bajar.

Mixed evidence debe explicitarse.

---

## 26. Data disagreement can itself be evidence

Puede indicar:

- segmentation;
- measurement issue;
- regime change.

---

## 27. Small Sample ≠ Zero Confidence

En small ecommerce puede ser razonable combinar:

- directional data;
- qualitative evidence;
- unit economics.

No imponer enterprise evidence standards.

---

## 28. Evidence Requirement Should Match Decision Consequence

Principio:

```text
Cheap + Reversible Decision
→ Lower Required Confidence
```

```text
Expensive + Irreversible Decision
→ Higher Required Confidence
```

---

## 29. Downside and Confidence interact

```text
Low Confidence
+
High Downside
=
Strong Reason for Caution
```

---

## 30. Confidence alone never removes Risk

Incluso high confidence puede requerir cautious rollout.

---

## 31. Cost of Error

Debe importar:

```text
Cost of Being Wrong
```

Puede existir asymmetry entre:

- false positive;
- false negative.

---

## 32. Error Asymmetry

Si actuar equivocadamente es muy costoso:

```text
Higher Confidence Needed
```

Si no actuar es mucho más costoso:

```text
Lower Confidence may be acceptable
```

---

## 33. Urgency changes threshold, not evidence strength

```text
Urgency
≠
More Confidence
```

Cambia:

```text
Decision Threshold
```

no:

```text
Evidence Strength
```

---

## 34. Reversibility

```text
Higher Reversibility
→
Lower Required Confidence
```

para initial action.

---

## 35. Blast Radius

Una acción enterprise-wide suele exigir mayor confidence que una acción limitada a un segmento.

---

## 36. Confidence-Aware Intervention Design

La IA no debe pensar solo:

```text
Do / Don't
```

Puede adaptar:

- scope;
- intensity;
- velocity;
- duration.

---

## 37. Maturity and Confidence

### Early Stage

Puede tener lower evidence density.

Debe poder actuar con:

- customer behavior;
- small tests;
- qualitative signals.

### Mature Organization

Puede exigir stronger:

- incrementality;
- segmentation;
- causal evidence.

Pero maturity afecta ability to gather evidence, no necesariamente la verdad subyacente.

---

## 38. Confidence ≠ Feasibility

```text
We know what to do
≠
We can do it now
```

---

## 39. Feasibility ≠ Confidence

Poder ejecutar algo fácilmente no significa que sea correcto.

---

## 40. Irreducible Uncertainty

A veces:

```text
More Evidence
is not obtainable
```

Entonces debe utilizarse:

```text
Robust Decision-Making
```

---

## 41. Robust vs Fragile Recommendation

### Robust

Funciona razonablemente en varios plausible scenarios.

### Fragile

Depende de una uncertain assumption muy concreta.

Bajo irreducible uncertainty, robust puede ser preferible aunque tenga menor theoretical upside.

---

## 42. Causal Chain Confidence

Una recommendation puede depender de:

```text
Problem
→ Driver
→ Cause
→ Lever
→ Intervention
```

Si un critical link es incierto:

```text
Recommendation Confidence ↓
```

---

## 43. No need for numerical probability

Puede usarse lenguaje conceptual:

- high;
- moderate;
- low;

sin convertirlo todavía en enum técnico.

---

## 44. Avoid Fake Precision

No inventar:

```text
73% confidence
```

sin probabilistic basis real.

```text
Precise Number
without precise evidence
=
False Confidence
```

---

## 45. Calibrated Wording

Puede expresarse como:

- Strong evidence suggests...
- The most likely explanation is...
- There is some evidence that...
- We do not yet know whether...

El lenguaje debe reflejar strength of evidence.

---

## 46. Confidence Must Be Explainable

No:

```text
Low Confidence
```

sin razón.

Mejor:

> Confidence is limited because we cannot separate traffic-quality deterioration from onsite conversion deterioration.

---

## 47. Factors Raising Confidence

Puede incluir:

- known causal defect;
- repeated observation;
- independent corroboration;
- reliable measurement;
- comparable historical evidence;
- clear mechanism.

---

## 48. Factors Lowering Confidence

Puede incluir:

- poor measurement;
- small sample;
- conflicting evidence;
- unclear cause;
- regime change;
- high heterogeneity;
- missing counterfactual.

---

## 49. Outcome Confidence ≠ Attribution Confidence

Puede existir:

```text
Outcome changed
```

con high confidence,

pero:

```text
intervention caused it
```

con low confidence.

---

## 50. Direction, Magnitude, Temporal, Durability, Scale Confidence

Pueden distinguirse:

```text
Directional Confidence
Magnitude Confidence
Temporal Confidence
Durability Confidence
Scale Confidence
```

---

## 51. Recommendation Confidence Is Multi-Layered

Preguntas útiles:

- Will it work?
- Will it matter?
- Will it last?
- Will it scale?
- Can we execute it?

No necesitan tener la misma respuesta.

---

## 52. Confidence and Time Horizon

Conceptualmente:

```text
Prediction Horizon ↑
→
Confidence ↓
```

frecuentemente.

No es universal.

---

## 53. Confidence does not replace Expected Outcome

Se necesitan ambos:

- what should happen;
- how sure are we.

---

## 54. Ideal Recommendation Structure

Puede incluir:

```text
Recommendation
Reason
Confidence
Main Uncertainty
Exposure Implication
```

---

## 55. Confidence Should Alter Recommendation Form

No basta añadir:

```text
Confidence: Low
```

después de recomendar full rollout.

La forma de la recomendación debe cambiar.

---

## 56. Confidence-Action Consistency

Conceptualmente:

```text
Low Confidence
→ Smaller Commitment
```

pero puede haber exceptions por:

- high cost of delay;
- high reversibility.

---

## 57. Confidence and No-action / Wait / Gather Evidence / Pilot / Execute / Scale

### Low confidence

Puede llevar a:

- test;
- wait;
- gather evidence;
- pilot;

según context.

### Sufficient confidence

Puede justificar:

- execute.

### Stronger confidence

Puede justificar:

- scale;

si marginal economics, risk y capacity también lo permiten.

---

## 58. Execution Confidence ≠ Scale Confidence

```text
Enough Evidence to Try
≠
Enough Evidence to Scale
```

---

## 59. Progressive Confidence

Conceptualmente:

```text
Hypothesis
↓
Directional Signal
↓
Validation
↓
Repeatability
↓
Scale Confidence
```

Y:

```text
Evidence
↓
Confidence
↓
Exposure
```

---

## 60. Confidence Is One Input, Not the Decision

Incluso high confidence puede quedar limitada por:

- constraints;
- risk;
- opportunity cost;
- low expected value.

---

## 61. Confidence and Portfolio

Una cartera robusta puede combinar:

```text
High-Confidence Core
+
Low-Confidence Exploration
```

Evitar dos extremos:

- solo uncertain bets;
- solo tiny high-confidence optimizations.

---

## 62. Confidence-Adjusted Expected Value

Una uncertain high-upside opportunity puede superar una certain tiny improvement.

Por tanto:

```text
Do Not Rank Solely by Confidence
```

---

## 63. Certainty Bias

Elegir siempre lo más seguro produce:

```text
Incrementalism
```

y puede hacer perder large opportunities.

---

## 64. Risk-Seeking Bias

El extremo opuesto:

```text
Always chase large uncertain opportunities
```

también es incorrecto.

---

## 65. Intervention Types Have Different Confidence Profiles

### Fix

Often higher confidence when defect known.

### Optimization

Depends on evidence.

### Experiment

Exists because confidence is insufficient.

### Pilot

Useful when strategic attractiveness exists but full-scale confidence is insufficient.

### Structural Transformation

May have high confidence in necessity but uncertain magnitude.

### Innovation

Often lower confidence by nature.

---

## 66. Experimentation Should Increase Decision Confidence

Un experiment valioso puede:

- raise confidence;
- lower confidence.

Ambos outcomes pueden ser útiles.

---

## 67. Prior Belief + New Evidence → Updated Belief

No requiere implementación bayesiana formal.

Pero:

```text
New Evidence
should change Confidence
proportionally to its strength
```

---

## 68. Strong vs Weak Contradictory Evidence

- strong contradiction → confidence falls materially;
- weak anomaly → should not erase a strong evidence base.

---

## 69. Regime Change

Context changes can reset confidence.

Examples:

- platform change;
- market shift;
- product change;
- pricing shift.

```text
Context Drift
→
Evidence Relevance ↓
```

---

## 70. Benchmarks as Priors, not Proof

Benchmark deviation puede aumentar diagnostic attention.

No demuestra automáticamente la intervención correcta.

---

## 71. Expert Knowledge as Prior Plausibility

Conceptually:

```text
Expert Knowledge
+
Local Evidence
→
Recommendation Confidence
```

Expertise debe narrow search space, no reemplazar evidence.

---

## 72. Authority Is Not Evidence

Popularity o expert consensus no sustituyen causal fit y local context.

---

## 73. Anecdote as Signal, not Proof

Anecdotes consistentes pueden aumentar confidence.

Pero:

```text
Stated Preference
≠
Revealed Preference
```

---

## 74. Quantitative + Qualitative Complementarity

Quantitative puede mostrar what.

Qualitative puede ayudar a explicar why.

Ambas pueden reforzarse.

---

## 75. Measurement Confidence

Si tracking es unreliable:

```text
Confidence ↓
```

aunque dashboard parezca preciso.

```text
Data Precision
≠
Data Accuracy
```

---

## 76. Missing Data

Missing data reduce confidence solo cuando son:

```text
Decision-Critical
```

No cualquier gap.

---

## 77. Proxy Confidence Should Not Transfer Automatically

Ejemplo:

```text
CTR improved
```

puede elevar confidence en:

```text
attention
```

pero no necesariamente en:

```text
profitable customer acquisition
```

---

## 78. Causal Distance Principle

Conceptualmente:

```text
More Causal Steps
→
More Uncertainty
```

---

## 79. Mechanism Confidence ≠ Tactic Confidence

Puede haber high confidence en:

```text
value perception is the lever
```

pero lower confidence en:

```text
bundle is best tactic
```

---

## 80. Granularity Affects Confidence

Más específica la claim:

```text
More Specific Claim
→
More Specific Evidence Required
```

---

## 81. Conditional Confidence

Future recommendations pueden depender de resultados previos.

```text
Fix Tracking
→
Then decide channel scaling
```

La confidence en step B debe actualizarse con evidence de step A.

---

## 82. Dependency Confidence

Una complex intervention con multiple assumptions puede estar limitada por su weakest critical assumption.

---

## 83. Complexity Creates Execution Uncertainty

```text
More Dependencies
→
Lower Robustness
```

frecuentemente.

---

## 84. Strategy Confidence ≠ Execution Confidence

```text
Strategy Confidence
=
idea is right
```

```text
Execution Confidence
=
organization can implement it
```

---

## 85. Practical Recommendation Confidence

Debe considerar ambas.

Una theoretically correct intervention con very low execution confidence no debería presentarse como high-confidence practical recommendation.

---

## 86. Trade-Off Confidence

Puede existir distinta confidence en:

- upside;
- sacrifice.

Ejemplo:

```text
Price increase:
High confidence margin/order rises
Lower confidence conversion response
```

Puede justificar selective rollout.

---

## 87. Confidence ≠ Variance

Se puede tener high confidence de que:

```text
outcome is highly variable
```

La distribución de downside importa.

---

## 88. Risk of Ruin

No expected upside justifica careless exposure a existential risk.

---

## 89. Expected Information Value

Si uncertainty es:

- decision-critical;
- reducible;
- cheap to resolve;

puede ser correcto:

```text
Gather Evidence
```

Si no puede reducirse barato:

```text
Choose Robust Action
```

---

## 90. Feedback Speed

Fast feedback puede permitir lower initial confidence.

Slow feedback + high commitment suele requerir stronger confidence upfront.

---

## 91. Optionality

Actions preserving:

```text
Future Options
```

pueden ser especialmente valiosas under uncertainty.

---

## 92. Sufficient Confidence

Objetivo:

```text
Enough Confidence
for
Current Commitment
```

No:

```text
Maximum Confidence
```

---

## 93. Confidence Threshold Is Contextual

Depende de:

- risk;
- reversibility;
- cost;
- scope;
- urgency;
- opportunity cost.

No hardcodear:

```text
Execute at 80% confidence
```

---

## 94. What We Know / Believe / Don't Know

Una salida experta puede distinguir:

### What We Know

Directly supported.

### What We Believe

Reasoned causal inference.

### What We Don't Know

Decision-critical uncertainty.

---

## 95. Confidence Should Be Actionable

Pregunta:

> ¿Qué cambia esta incertidumbre sobre lo que debemos hacer?

---

## 96. Confidence → Action Implication

No:

```text
Confidence: Medium
```

solo.

Mejor:

> Confidence is moderate, so test on limited scope before full rollout.

---

## 97. Portfolio Confidence Correlation

Varias low-confidence bets no diversifican risk si dependen del mismo uncertain diagnosis.

Shared assumptions matter.

---

## 98. Confidence Is Dynamic

```text
Confidence Today
≠
Confidence Forever
```

Debe subir o bajar con evidence.

---

## 99. Confirmation Bias

La IA debe buscar:

> What would prove us wrong?

---

## 100. Confidence Must Be Falsifiable

Si nada puede bajar confidence:

> el razonamiento no está calibrado.

---

## 101. Layer-Specific Confidence Update

Si mechanism funcionó pero final outcome fue bloqueado:

```text
Mechanism Confidence ↑
Scale Confidence may ↓
```

No reducir confidence en la capa equivocada.

---

## 102. Invalid Evidence

Poor implementation o broken measurement no deberían materially update strategy confidence.

---

## 103. Evidence Source Literacy

La IA debe entender qué puede y qué no puede demostrar:

- customer research;
- reviews;
- support tickets;
- heatmaps;
- attribution platforms;
- experiments.

---

## 104. Experiment ≠ Truth

Poor experiment design puede inducir false confidence.

Distinguir:

- internal validity;
- external validity.

Scale confidence requiere más que pilot confidence.

---

## 105. Structural vs Tactical Confidence

Tactical actions suelen tener:

- faster feedback;
- lower commitment.

Structural actions:

- longer horizon;
- more dependencies.

Puede haber high confidence en direction y low confidence en exact impact.

---

## 106. Confidence Communication

Puede estructurarse como:

```text
Recommendation
Confidence
Why
Main Uncertainty
Implication
```

---

## 107. Communicate Decision-Relevant Uncertainty Only

No listar todas las uncertainties.

Identificar:

> la principal incertidumbre capaz de cambiar la recomendación.

---

## 108. Strong / Moderate / Low Language

### High

> I recommend fixing X first.

### Moderate

> This is the most likely next move, but validate it on limited scope first.

### Low

> I would not commit yet. The key uncertainty is...

---

## 109. Clear Action + Honest Uncertainty

Evitar:

```text
certainty theater
```

y:

```text
paralysis theater
```

---

## 110. Confidence Cannot Rescue Bad Economics

High confidence + low expected value sigue sin justificar priority.

---

## 111. Confidence Cannot Rescue Infeasibility

High confidence + no resources sigue sin ser executable.

---

## 112. Confidence Cannot Override Guardrails

Una high-confidence tactic que viola safety/legal constraints no se recomienda.

---

## 113. Calibration Over Time

Futuro learning system puede comparar:

```text
Predicted Confidence
vs
Actual Outcomes
```

para detectar:

- overconfidence;
- underconfidence.


---

## Principio maestro

La IA no debe intentar aparentar certeza.

Debe estar:

> tan segura como permite la evidencia y tan prudente como exige el coste de equivocarse.

La lógica final es:

```text
Evidence
→ Confidence
→ Commitment
→ New Evidence
→ Updated Confidence
→ Updated Commitment
```

Regla central:

```text
Do not wait for certainty.
Do not pretend certainty.
Commit in proportion to what you actually know.
```
