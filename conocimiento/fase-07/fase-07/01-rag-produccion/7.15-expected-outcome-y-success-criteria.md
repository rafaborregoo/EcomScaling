# FASE 7.15 — Expected Outcome y Success Criteria

## Propósito

Una recomendación no está completa cuando la IA solo explica:

> qué hacer.

También debe explicar:

> qué debería ocurrir si la intervención funciona

y:

> qué evidencia sería suficiente para considerar que ha funcionado suficientemente.

Principio:

```text
Recommendation
without
Expected Outcome
=
Incomplete Recommendation
```

---

## 1. Expected Outcome

Expected Outcome significa:

> el cambio que razonablemente esperamos observar si el mecanismo causal de la intervención funciona.

No es:

- guaranteed outcome;
- promise;
- forecast with false precision.

Es:

```text
Reasoned Expectation
```

---

## 2. Success Criteria

Success Criteria responde:

> ¿Qué evidencia sería suficiente para considerar que la intervención ha producido valor?

Esto debe ayudar posteriormente a decidir:

- Stop;
- Continue;
- Scale;
- Rollback.

---

## 3. Expected Outcome ≠ Success Criteria

```text
Expected Outcome
=
What should happen
```

```text
Success Criteria
=
What evidence would make us
consider the intervention successful
```

---

## 4. Causal Alignment

El outcome esperado debe seguir:

```text
Intervention
→ Mechanism
→ Expected Change
```

No:

```text
Do X
→ Revenue goes up somehow
```

---

## 5. Causal Outcome Chain

Pensar:

```text
Intervention
↓
Immediate Mechanism
↓
Behavioral / Operational Change
↓
Business Outcome
```

---

## 6. Mechanism-Level Outcome

No limitar outcome a:

- increase CVR;
- increase AOV;
- increase ROAS.

Eso describe una métrica, no necesariamente la causalidad.

Mejor:

```text
Customers understand bundle value more clearly
→ more multi-item orders
→ AOV may improve
```

---

## 7. Leading Outcome vs Final Business Outcome

### Leading Outcome

Muestra que el mechanism puede estar activándose.

### Final Business Outcome

Debe reflejar valor:

- economic;
- strategic;
- operational;
- capability;
- risk reduction.

```text
Proxy Improvement
≠
Business Success
```

---

## 8. Outcome Hierarchy

Puede pensarse:

```text
Mechanism Outcome
↓
Behavior Outcome
↓
Economic Outcome
↓
Strategic Outcome
```

No toda intervención necesita las cuatro capas.

---

## 9. Intervention-Type Outcomes

### Known Defect

Éxito puede ser simplemente:

```text
defect disappears
and normal function returns
```

### Capability Intervention

Puede producir:

```text
better measurement
better decision quality
```

sin revenue inmediato.

### Defensive Intervention

Puede producir:

```text
risk reduced
fragility reduced
loss prevented
```

### Learning Intervention

Puede producir:

```text
decision-critical uncertainty reduced
```

---

## 10. Direct vs Indirect Outcome

### Direct

Surge directamente del mechanism.

### Indirect

Aparece después.

Ejemplo:

```text
Better Data
→ Better Allocation
→ Better Economics
```

---

## 11. Preservation Is an Outcome

A veces success significa:

```text
Prevent Deterioration
```

no:

```text
Improve Metric
```

Flat performance puede ser éxito si el counterfactual era deterioro material.

---

## 12. Counterfactual Thinking

Siempre preguntar:

> Compared to what?

Absolute outcome puede engañar.

---

## 13. Expected Outcome Is Not Guaranteed Outcome

Usar lenguaje condicional:

```text
If the causal assumptions hold,
we would expect...
```

---

## 14. Direction vs Magnitude

```text
Direction
=
what should improve
```

```text
Magnitude
=
how much
```

Puede haber high confidence en direction y low confidence en magnitude.

---

## 15. Do Not Invent Magnitudes

No inventar:

- +10%;
- +20%;
- 2x;

sin evidencia.

Un outcome cualitativo puede ser mejor que false precision.

---

## 16. Range Only When Justified

Puede existir rango contextual si hay:

- historical evidence;
- benchmark;
- test history.

Nunca universal.

---

## 17. Success Thresholds Are Contextual

No existe regla como:

```text
CVR must improve 10%
```

para todos.

El threshold depende de:

- economics;
- implementation cost;
- risk;
- opportunity cost;
- maturity;
- baseline;
- headroom.

---

## 18. Improvement ≠ Successful Investment

Una métrica puede mejorar y aun así no justificar:

- cost;
- risk;
- opportunity cost.

La pregunta correcta:

> ¿Es el cambio material?

---

## 19. Statistical Significance ≠ Business Significance

Una variación detectable puede tener negligible economic value.

Y una señal económicamente importante puede tener limited statistical certainty en negocios pequeños.

---

## 20. Success Criteria Must Include Guardrails

No basta:

```text
Target KPI improves
```

También:

```text
without unacceptable damage elsewhere
```

---

## 21. Primary Success Criterion + Guardrails

### Primary

Qué necesita lograr principalmente la intervención.

### Guardrails

Qué no debe deteriorarse de forma inaceptable.

---

## 22. System-Level Success

```text
Local KPI Improvement
≠
System Success
```

Debe preguntarse:

> Did business value improve overall?

---

## 23. Success Can Include Trade-Off

No todas las dimensiones deben mejorar.

Puede existir:

```text
CVR ↓ slightly
Margin ↑ materially
```

y ser success si el trade-off es:

- intentional;
- understood;
- economically justified.

---

## 24. Risk-Adjusted Success

Preguntar:

> Was additional value worth additional risk?

Resilience puede formar parte de success.

---

## 25. Expected Outcome and Time Horizon

Puede existir:

- immediate;
- intermediate;
- delayed.

Success Criteria deben usar un horizon correcto.

---

## 26. Evaluation Window

Debe ser suficientemente largo para que:

```text
the causal mechanism
has time to manifest
```

Pero no tan largo que mantenga exposure claramente dañina.

---

## 27. Leading Indicators

Pueden ayudar cuando final outcome tarda.

Pero:

```text
Leading Indicator
≠
Final Validation
```

---

## 28. Metric Hierarchy

Puede pensarse:

```text
Diagnostic Metric
Leading Metric
Business Metric
Guardrail
```

---

## 29. Measurement Convenience Bias

No medir solo lo fácil.

Medir lo que realmente informa la decisión.

---

## 30. Success Criteria Must Match Intervention Type

### Known Defect

```text
defect resolved
```

### Experiment

```text
hypothesis receives enough support
to justify next decision
```

### Pilot

```text
value appears under contained exposure
and operational viability is demonstrated
```

### Optimization

```text
incremental value
exceeds implementation + opportunity cost
```

### Structural Change

```text
new operating state
is sufficiently better and stable
```

### Capability Building

```text
business can perform
previously unavailable valuable actions
```

### Scaling

```text
additional exposure
creates acceptable marginal value
```

### Stabilization

```text
fragility / failure / variance reduce sufficiently
```

### Defensive

```text
material downside prevented or reduced
```

### Learning

```text
decision certainty improves
```

---

## 31. Learning Success ≠ Commercial Success

Un experimento puede ser successful learning aunque la new variant pierda.

```text
Hypothesis rejected
≠
Experiment failure
```

si produce decision-relevant learning.

---

## 32. Capability Success

No medir capability building solo por revenue.

Puede medirse por:

```text
decisions unlocked
```

---

## 33. Expected Outcome Should Be Observable

No:

```text
Improve the brand
```

Mejor:

- stronger preference;
- more branded demand;
- improved willingness to choose;
- better pricing power;

cuando causalmente justificado.

---

## 34. Observable ≠ Immediately Quantifiable

Puede existir evidence:

- qualitative;
- behavioral;
- operational.

---

## 35. Success Criteria Should Be Falsifiable

Debe ser posible concluir:

```text
This did not work sufficiently
```

Si cualquier outcome puede reinterpretarse como success, el criterio es inútil.

---

## 36. Predefine Success / Failure / Ambiguous

Conceptualmente:

```text
Success Evidence
Failure Evidence
Ambiguous Evidence
```

Ambiguous es válido.

Pero no para siempre.

---

## 37. Success Criteria Are Decision Criteria

Deben ayudar a decidir:

- Scale;
- Continue;
- Stop;
- Rollback.

No son simplemente reporting metrics.

---

## 38. Pilot Success ≠ Full-Scale Success

```text
Works Small
≠
Works Large
```

Y:

```text
Success at one scope
≠
Transferability
```

---

## 39. Scope-Specific Outcomes

Puede existir:

- success for segment A;
- failure for segment B.

Evitar averages que oculten heterogeneity.

Pero también evitar over-segmentation.

---

## 40. Incrementality

Especialmente en growth:

```text
Attributed Outcome
≠
Incremental Outcome
```

Preguntar:

> Did the intervention create additional business value?

---

## 41. Pull-Forward and Cannibalization

Success window debe capturar, cuando sea material:

- future demand pulled forward;
- channel/product cannibalization.

---

## 42. Gross Lift ≠ Net Lift

Expected Outcome debe ser net-aware.

---

## 43. Marginal Economics

Scaling intervention debe juzgar:

```text
next unit economics
```

no average historical performance.

---

## 44. Acceptable Deterioration

Puede ser racional aceptar peor:

- ROAS;
- CVR;
- efficiency metric;

si net contribution mejora.

---

## 45. Revenue Growth ≠ Economic Success

Según contexto evaluar:

- gross margin;
- contribution;
- cash;
- payback;
- retention quality.

---

## 46. Multi-Objective Success

Puede estructurarse como:

```text
Primary Objective
+
Acceptable Trade-offs
+
Guardrails
```

---

## 47. Sustainability

Success debe considerar:

> Can this result persist under normal operating conditions?

---

## 48. Repeatability

Un one-time result puede ser:

```text
Signal
```

no:

```text
System
```

---

## 49. Durability

Preguntar:

> How long is the value likely to persist?

Pequeña mejora durable puede superar un spike breve.

---

## 50. Maintenance Cost

Una intervención puede funcionar y aun así tener poor net operational value.

Preguntar:

> Is ongoing value worth ongoing maintenance?

---

## 51. Adoption

En tools/processes:

```text
Implementation
≠
Adoption
```

y:

```text
Adoption
≠
Value
```

---

## 52. Capacity Creation

Automation/capability interventions pueden tener éxito si:

- time saved;
- errors reduced;
- constrained capacity released.

---

## 53. Resilience

Intervenciones defensivas pueden medir:

- dependency reduction;
- redundancy;
- blast-radius reduction.

No necesitan immediate revenue lift.

---

## 54. Brand / SEO / Content / Creative

No usar vanity metrics como final success.

### SEO

Ranking y traffic pueden ser intermediate outcomes.

### Content

Views ≠ commercial value.

### Creative

Attention ≠ profitable demand.

---

## 55. Product / Pricing / Promotions

### Product

Consider:

- demand;
- margin;
- returns;
- repeat;
- cannibalization.

### Pricing

Puede priorizar:

```text
Net Contribution
```

sobre CVR aislado.

### Promotions

Consider:

- incremental demand;
- margin;
- pull-forward;
- cannibalization.

---

## 56. Inventory and Operations

### Inventory

Success puede ser:

- cash released;
- inventory risk reduced.

### Operations

Success puede ser:

- throughput improved;
- error rate controlled;
- service quality protected.

---

## 57. Goodhart-like Risk

Cuando una métrica aislada se convierte en objetivo:

```text
metric optimization
can replace
system optimization
```

Success Criteria deben ser hard to game.

---

## 58. Outcome Proximity

Cuanto más lejos en la causal chain:

```text
Attribution Confidence ↓
```

Puede usarse:

```text
Near Mechanism Outcome
+
Business Outcome
```

---

## 59. External Factors

Context-adjusted evaluation debe considerar cambios externos.

Pero no usar “the market” como excusa sin evidence.

---

## 60. Expected Outcome as Hypothesis

Formato mental:

```text
If we do X
because Y is true,
we expect Z.
```

---

## 61. Success Criteria as Decision Rule

Conceptualmente:

```text
We consider this successful
if the expected mechanism appears
and net business effect is sufficiently positive
without violating key guardrails.
```

---

## 62. Specificity Without False Precision

Es suficiente especificar:

- direction;
- mechanism;
- guardrail;
- horizon.

Sin número inventado.

---

## 63. Confidence and Exposure

Low confidence puede hacer que success inicial sea:

```text
evidence becomes clearer
```

High confidence puede exigir:

```text
business outcome appears as expected
```

Confidence afecta exposure y evidence requirement, no debe redefinir arbitrariamente success.

---

## 64. No-action / Wait / Gather Evidence Outcomes

Incluso estas decisiones pueden tener Expected Outcome.

### No-action

```text
maintain healthy state
while resources focus elsewhere
```

### Wait

```text
relevant uncertainty resolves naturally
```

### Gather Evidence

```text
decision-critical uncertainty decreases
```

---

## 65. Partial Success

Puede significar:

```text
mechanism moved
but downstream bottleneck limited business outcome
```

Esto puede revelar next constraint.

---

## 66. Diagnostic Feedback

Outcome también informa sobre causal model.

### Mechanism does not move

Hypothesis may be wrong.

### Mechanism moves, business outcome does not

Downstream constraint may exist.

### Outcome moves without mechanism

Causal story may be incomplete.

---

## 67. Recommendation as Controlled Learning

Toda execution produce información sobre causal thesis.

---

## 68. Pre-Commitment Prevents Goalpost Moving

Antes de ejecución, definir cuando sea practical:

- success;
- failure;
- inconclusive.

No redefinir target después de observar results.

---

## 69. Unexpected Outcomes

Unexpected positive/negative outcomes deben registrarse como unexpected.

No reescribir la tesis original.

---

## 70. Success ≠ Still Highest Priority

Una intervención puede superar criteria y aun así no merecer más resources.

Opportunity cost continúa importando.

---

## 71. Graduation

Una intervention puede:

```text
meet success criteria
→ become BAU
→ release strategic resources
```

---

## 72. Good Enough

Success puede significar:

```text
sufficiently solved for now
```

No perfect.

Cuando:

```text
next improvement
<
next best opportunity
```

mover recursos.

---

## 73. Portfolio-Level Success

No double-count impacts.

Puede existir:

- overlap;
- synergy;
- cannibalization.

La cartera debe evaluarse por:

```text
Net Combined Value
```

---

## 74. Constraint-Adjusted Outcome

Reduced scope debe producir reduced expected outcome.

```text
Actual Scope
+
Actual Resources
+
Actual Intensity
→
Feasible Expected Outcome
```

---

## 75. Maturity-Aware Success

### Early

Learning puede ser primary outcome.

### Repeatable

Efficiency / standardization.

### Scaling

Marginal economics.

### Mature

Marginal improvement / resilience / allocation.

---

## 76. Cash-Aware Success

Una intervention rentable puede fallar si cash requirement es unsustainable.

No existe universal payback threshold.

---

## 77. Sustainable Success

No depende de:

```text
heroic effort
```

Debe poder reproducirse bajo normal operations.

---

## 78. Short-Term vs Long-Term

No matar long-term strategy por short-term metric.

Pero tampoco usar “long term” para justificar bad results indefinidamente.

Usar:

```text
Early Signals
→ Intermediate Evidence
→ Long-Term Outcome
```

---

## 79. Planned Cost ≠ Failure

Algunas structural interventions tienen temporary cost.

Debe estar anticipated y dentro de acceptable bounds.

---

## 80. Scenario Thinking

Puede haber:

- expected case;
- upside case;
- downside case;

conceptualmente.

Sin fake forecast tables.

---

## 81. Robust vs Fragile Success

### Robust

Funciona bajo varios plausible scenarios.

### Fragile

Solo funciona si una narrow assumption es cierta.

---

## 82. Dependency-Aware Success

Una intervention puede funcionar en su component y fallar a system level por dependency.

No confundir:

- component success;
- intervention success;
- portfolio success;
- business success.

---

## 83. Implementation Fidelity

Antes de declarar failure:

> ¿Se ejecutó realmente como estaba diseñada?

Low fidelity reduce inference quality.

---

## 84. Executability Is Part of Practical Success

Una theoretically good intervention que nobody can execute puede ser mala practical recommendation.

---

## 85. Success ≠ Scale Readiness

Un successful pilot puede requerir capability build antes de Scale.

---

## 86. Failure Criteria

Puede incluir:

- mechanism absent;
- economics deteriorate;
- guardrail breach;
- no meaningful impact.

Small positive result todavía puede ser failure si cost es demasiado alto.

---

## 87. Inconclusive Criteria

Puede existir cuando:

- direction unclear;
- measurement unreliable;
- window incomplete.

Debe llevar a:

- continue;
- gather evidence;
- stop if learning cost too high.

---

## 88. Data Quality

Tracking integrity y metric definition consistency forman parte del contexto de evaluación.

---

## 89. No Universal KPI Set

Cada intervention necesita relevant evidence.

`KPI laundry list` es mala consultoría.

---

## 90. Communication Compression

Una recommendation fuerte puede resumirse como:

```text
Expected Outcome:
X should happen because Y.

Success:
We consider this successful if Z,
provided guardrail G does not materially worsen.
```

---

## 91. Expected Outcome = Contract with Reality

Conceptualmente:

```text
We believe X.
Reality should show Y.
If it does not,
we update.
```

---

## 92. Expected vs Observed Gap

Puede revelar:

- causal model error;
- implementation failure;
- external shock;
- hidden constraint.

El gap es información.

---

## 93. Master Mental Model

```text
Recommendation
↓
Causal Mechanism
↓
Expected Outcome
↓
Observable Evidence
↓
Primary Success Criterion
+
Guardrails
↓
Observed Result
↓
Stop / Continue / Scale / Rollback
```

---

## 94. Expected Outcome Construction

Preguntar:

1. What cause are we addressing?
2. What lever are we changing?
3. What should change first?
4. What business outcome should follow?
5. Over what horizon?
6. What could deteriorate elsewhere?
7. What evidence would count as sufficient?
8. What outcome would change the next decision?

---

## 95. Success Criteria Construction

Conceptualmente:

```text
Primary Business Outcome
+
Mechanism Evidence
+
Guardrails
+
Time Context
+
Materiality
```

No necesariamente todo debe mostrarse explícitamente cada vez.

---

## 96. Core Questions

- Is the improvement material?
- Is it incremental?
- Is it economically valuable?
- Is it sustainable?
- Is it repeatable?
- Is it scalable?
- Did we damage something else?
- Is attribution credible?
- Is this still best use of resources?
- Is the area Good Enough?

---

## 97. Principles to Preserve

```text
Expected Outcome ≠ Guaranteed Outcome
Expected Outcome ≠ Success Criteria
Metric Movement ≠ Business Success
Proxy ≠ Outcome
Revenue Growth ≠ Economic Success
Statistical Significance ≠ Business Significance
Improvement ≠ Material Improvement
Pilot Success ≠ Scale Readiness
Local Success ≠ System Success
Attributed Revenue ≠ Incremental Revenue
Gross Lift ≠ Net Lift
Completion ≠ Success
Usage ≠ Value
Learning Failure ≠ Commercial Failure
One-Time Success ≠ Repeatable Success
Repeatable Success ≠ Scalable Success
Short-Term Win ≠ Long-Term Win
Primary Metric Win ≠ Portfolio Win
```

---

## Principio maestro

La función del Expected Outcome no es predecir el futuro con falsa precisión.

Es hacer explícita la tesis causal de la recomendación.

Y la función de Success Criteria no es poner números arbitrarios.

Es definir qué evidencia sería suficiente para poder afirmar:

> Esta intervención ha creado suficiente valor como para mantenerla, ampliarla o considerarla resuelta.

```text
Recommendation
↓
Causal Expectation
↓
Observed Reality
↓
Net Business Value
↓
Decision
```
