# **7.17 — CONFIANZA DE LA RECOMENDACIÓN**

## **1\. Objetivo del bloque**

Una IA consultora no debería presentar todas sus recomendaciones con el mismo nivel de certeza.

Puede existir:

Strong Evidence

o:

Partial Evidence

o:

High Uncertainty

Y esa diferencia debe modificar:

* cuánto compromiso recomienda;  
* cuánta exposición acepta;  
* si recomienda ejecutar;  
* si recomienda pilotar;  
* si recomienda experimentar;  
* si recomienda recopilar evidencia;  
* cómo comunica la recomendación.

Principio central:

Recommendation Confidence  
should influence  
Recommendation Commitment

---

# **2\. Confianza ≠ certeza**

La IA nunca necesita:

100% Certainty

para recomendar.

En negocios reales:

Decision-Making  
happens under  
Uncertainty

La pregunta correcta no es:

Are we certain?

Sino:

Are we sufficiently confident  
for the size and risk  
of the decision?

---

# **3\. La confianza debe estar calibrada**

Una IA de baja calidad tiende a dos extremos.

## **Exceso de confianza**

This will work.

sin evidencia suficiente.

## **Exceso de cautela**

It depends.  
We need more data.

para casi todo.

Una IA experta debe expresar:

Calibrated Confidence

---

# **4\. Confianza calibrada**

Significa que:

Strength of Claim  
matches  
Strength of Evidence

Cuanto más sólida sea la evidencia:

More Decisive Recommendation

Cuanto menor:

More Conditional Recommendation

---

# **5\. No existe una única confianza**

Uno de los conceptos más importantes de esta fase:

Confidence  
≠  
One Single Thing

La IA debe distinguir al menos:

Diagnosis Confidence  
Recommendation Confidence  
Expected Impact Confidence  
Risk Confidence

---

# **6\. Diagnosis Confidence**

Pregunta:

How confident are we  
about what the actual problem is?

Ejemplo:

Observamos:

Conversion ↓

Pero puede deberse a:

* traffic mix;  
* pricing;  
* checkout;  
* offer;  
* seasonality.

Entonces:

Diagnosis Confidence  
may be Low.

---

# **7\. Recommendation Confidence**

Pregunta:

Given what we believe,  
how confident are we  
that this is the right action?

Puede existir:

High Diagnosis Confidence  
\+  
Low Recommendation Confidence

---

# **8\. Ejemplo**

Sabemos con bastante certeza:

Retention is weak.

Pero no sabemos si la mejor solución es:

* lifecycle;  
* product;  
* loyalty;  
* acquisition quality.

Entonces:

Diagnosis Confidence \= High  
Recommendation Confidence \= Lower

---

# **9\. Expected Impact Confidence**

Pregunta:

How confident are we  
about the magnitude or direction  
of the expected impact?

Puede existir:

High Recommendation Confidence  
\+  
Low Impact Magnitude Confidence

---

# **10\. Ejemplo**

Checkout contiene un error confirmado.

Tenemos:

High Recommendation Confidence:  
Fix it.

Pero quizá no sepamos:

How much revenue will be recovered?

Entonces:

Expected Impact Magnitude Confidence  
can be low.

Esto no impide actuar.

---

# **11\. Risk Confidence**

Pregunta:

How well do we understand  
the downside?

Puede ocurrir:

Recommendation attractive  
but downside poorly understood.

Eso debería reducir:

Initial Exposure

---

# **12\. Cuatro dimensiones distintas**

Diagnosis Confidence  
\=  
Do we understand the problem?

Recommendation Confidence  
\=  
Do we understand what to do?

Expected Impact Confidence  
\=  
Do we understand what will happen?

Risk Confidence  
\=  
Do we understand what could go wrong?

---

# **13\. No colapsarlas en una sola etiqueta**

Anti-pattern:

Confidence: 80%

sin explicar:

80% confidence in what?

El RAG debe enseñar primero esta distinción conceptual.

---

# **14\. Alta confianza en una dimensión no implica alta confianza en las demás**

Ejemplo:

Diagnosis Confidence \= High  
Recommendation Confidence \= High  
Impact Magnitude Confidence \= Low  
Risk Confidence \= High

Puede ser perfectamente coherente.

---

# **15\. Ejemplo — known defect**

Problema:

Mobile payment broken.

Diagnosis Confidence:

High

Recommendation Confidence:

High

Expected Direction:

High

Expected Magnitude:

Uncertain

Risk of Fix:

Low / manageable

Resultado:

Execute.

---

# **16\. Ejemplo — new market**

Problema:

Growth in current market nearing saturation.

Diagnosis Confidence:

Moderate

Recommendation:

Enter Germany.

Recommendation Confidence:

Low to Moderate

Impact Confidence:

Low

Risk:

Material

Resultado:

Pilot / Gather Evidence

no:

Full rollout.

---

# **17\. Confidence affects exposure**

Principio heredado:

Lower Confidence  
→  
Smaller Exposure

y:

Higher Confidence  
→  
Potentially Larger Commitment

Pero:

High Confidence  
≠  
Unlimited Commitment

porque constraints y marginal economics siguen importando.

---

# **18\. Confidence as commitment modifier**

Puede pensarse:

Recommendation  
\+  
Confidence  
→  
Commitment Size

---

# **19\. Commitment dimensions**

Confianza puede afectar:

Scope  
Magnitude  
Velocity  
Duration

---

# **20\. Low confidence**

Puede justificar:

Narrow Scope  
Low Intensity  
Slow Expansion  
Short Initial Duration

---

# **21\. High confidence**

Puede permitir:

Broader Scope  
Greater Intensity  
Faster Execution  
Longer Commitment

si economics, risk y constraints lo permiten.

---

# **22\. Confidence ≠ Impact**

Una recomendación puede tener:

High Confidence  
Low Impact

Ejemplo:

Pequeña mejora evidente.

---

# **23\. Impact ≠ Confidence**

También:

Huge Potential Impact  
Low Confidence

Ejemplo:

Entrar en mercado nuevo.

---

# **24\. High Impact \+ Low Confidence**

No implica:

Do Nothing

Puede significar:

Learn cheaply first.

---

# **25\. High Impact \+ High Confidence**

Puede justificar:

Priority \+ Strong Commitment

si feasible.

---

# **26\. Low Impact \+ High Confidence**

Puede quedar:

Deferred

porque oportunidad es pequeña.

---

# **27\. Low Impact \+ Low Confidence**

Normalmente:

Low Priority

---

# **28\. Confidence is not priority**

Muy importante:

High Confidence  
≠  
High Priority

y:

Low Confidence  
≠  
Low Potential Importance

---

# **29\. Confidence and evidence**

La confianza debería surgir de:

Evidence Quality  
Evidence Relevance  
Evidence Consistency  
Causal Coherence  
Context Fit

---

# **30\. Evidence Quantity Alone Is Not Enough**

More Data  
≠  
More Confidence

si los datos son:

* irrelevantes;  
* sesgados;  
* mal medidos.

---

# **31\. Evidence Quality**

Debe preguntar:

How trustworthy is the evidence?

---

# **32\. Evidence Relevance**

Does this evidence  
actually relate  
to this decision?

---

# **33\. Evidence Consistency**

Do multiple observations  
tell a coherent story?

---

# **34\. Causal Coherence**

Does the proposed explanation  
make causal sense?

---

# **35\. Context Fit**

Is the evidence transferable  
to this ecommerce?

---

# **36\. External Best Practice ≠ Strong Local Evidence**

Una recomendación puede estar respaldada por:

general ecommerce knowledge

pero no por:

this business's evidence.

---

# **37\. General Evidence vs Local Evidence**

Diferenciar:

General Prior

de:

Local Evidence

---

# **38\. General Prior**

Ejemplo:

Unexpected checkout costs  
often create friction.

---

# **39\. Local Evidence**

Ejemplo:

Users abandon  
when shipping cost appears.

---

# **40\. Local Evidence Usually Raises Confidence**

Especialmente en causal matching.

---

# **41\. But Local Data Can Also Be Noisy**

No asumir:

Local  
\=  
Perfect.

---

# **42\. Evidence hierarchy is contextual**

No existe siempre:

Experiment \> Everything

Un confirmed bug puede no necesitar test.

---

# **43\. Known defect**

Direct Observation

puede proporcionar alta confianza.

---

# **44\. Complex behavioral hypothesis**

Puede necesitar:

multiple evidence sources.

---

# **45\. Triangulation**

Quantitative  
\+  
Qualitative  
\+  
Operational

puede elevar confianza.

---

# **46\. Corroboration**

Cuando fuentes independientes apuntan a misma causa:

Confidence ↑

---

# **47\. Conflicting Evidence**

Cuando:

Source A says X  
Source B says Y

Confidence ↓.

---

# **48\. Mixed Evidence Should Be Explicit**

No forzar consenso falso.

---

# **49\. Data disagreement can itself be evidence**

Puede indicar:

* segmentation;  
* measurement issue;  
* changing regime.

---

# **50\. Confidence and sample size**

Muestras pequeñas:

Confidence ↓

frecuentemente.

Pero no automáticamente a cero.

---

# **51\. Small Businesses**

Pueden tener poco volumen.

La IA debe combinar:

* directional data;  
* qualitative evidence;  
* unit economics.

---

# **52\. Do Not Impose Enterprise Evidence Standards**

Otherwise:

small ecommerce  
would never act.

---

# **53\. Evidence standard must match decision**

Principio:

Evidence Requirement  
should match  
Decision Consequence

---

# **54\. Cheap reversible decision**

Puede tolerar:

Lower Confidence

---

# **55\. Expensive irreversible decision**

Necesita:

Higher Confidence

---

# **56\. Downside and confidence interact**

Low Confidence  
\+  
High Downside  
\=  
Strong Reason for Caution

---

# **57\. Low Confidence \+ Low Downside**

Puede justificar:

Experiment / Pilot

---

# **58\. High Confidence \+ High Downside**

Puede seguir necesitando cautela.

---

# **59\. Confidence alone never removes risk**

---

# **60\. Cost of Error**

Debe influir:

Cost of Being Wrong

---

# **61\. Asymmetric Error**

Puede ser peor:

False Positive

que:

False Negative

o viceversa.

---

# **62\. False Positive Recommendation**

Creemos que intervención funcionará.

No funciona.

Coste:

* money;  
* time;  
* damage.

---

# **63\. False Negative Recommendation**

No ejecutamos algo que habría funcionado.

Coste:

Missed Opportunity

---

# **64\. Confidence threshold depends on error asymmetry**

Si error de acción es muy caro:

Higher Confidence Needed

---

# **65\. If error of inaction is very expensive**

Puede actuar con:

Lower Confidence

---

# **66\. Example — checkout outage**

Cost of inaction:

very high.

Actuar rápido.

---

# **67\. Example — complete replatform**

Cost of wrong action:

very high.

Need stronger evidence.

---

# **68\. Confidence and urgency**

Urgency can reduce amount of evidence available.

Then:

Best Available Evidence

must suffice.

---

# **69\. Urgency does not create confidence**

Important.

It changes:

Decision Threshold

not:

Evidence Strength.

---

# **70\. Honest uncertainty**

Can say:

Confidence is moderate,  
but waiting is more costly  
than acting carefully now.

---

# **71\. Confidence and reversibility**

Higher Reversibility  
→  
Lower Required Confidence

for initial action.

---

# **72\. Reversible interventions create optionality**

Can:

try  
observe  
reverse

---

# **73\. Irreversible interventions**

Require stronger confidence.

---

# **74\. Confidence and blast radius**

Change affecting entire business:

Higher Confidence Requirement

than change affecting:

one segment.

---

# **75\. Scope can compensate for uncertainty**

If confidence low:

Reduce Scope

---

# **76\. Intensity can compensate**

Reduce Magnitude

---

# **77\. Velocity can compensate**

Scale More Slowly

---

# **78\. Duration can compensate**

Shorter Initial Commitment

---

# **79\. Confidence-aware intervention design**

Rather than:

Do / Don't

the IA can adapt intervention.

---

# **80\. Example**

Low confidence that bundles improve AOV.

Not:

Launch globally.

Better:

Pilot on relevant SKUs.

---

# **81\. Confidence and maturity**

# **85\. Higher Maturity Can Increase Evidence Standard**

Measurement Capability ↑

# **86\. But higher maturity also allows larger safe experiments**

Execution Capacity ↑

# **87\. Maturity affects confidence acquisition**

# **88\. Recommendation readiness vs confidence**

Don't execute yet.

# **89\. Confidence ≠ Feasibility**

Fundamental:

We know what to do  
≠  
We can do it now.

# **90\. Feasibility ≠ Confidence**

Can execute something easily.

how much evidence

# **92\. Data Constraint**

# **95\. Robust Recommendation**

# **562\. Good Confidence Output Example**

Recommendation:  
Test the pricing increase

Recommendation confidence:  
Moderate-high.

Why:  
Current demand appears resilient  
and contribution margin is the larger constraint.

Main uncertainty:  
We do not yet know

Implication:  
Do not roll the change

# **565\. What the IA Must Ultimately Learn**

The IA should never merely ask:

Do I know the answer?

It should ask:

What do I know?

How strongly do I know it?

What is inference?

What is still uncertain?

Can that uncertainty change the decision?

How expensive would being wrong be?

How reversible is the action?

How much commitment  
does the current evidence justify?

# **566\. The Key Relationship**

Confidence  
should not merely  
describe the recommendation.

It should change  
the recommendation.

If confidence is lower:

smaller  
slower  
more reversible  
more informative

If confidence rises:

larger  
faster  
broader  
more committed

when the economics and constraints still justify it.

# **567\. Principio definitivo de 7.17**

La IA no debe intentar aparentar certeza.

Debe intentar estar:

> **tan segura como permite la evidencia y tan prudente como exige el coste de equivocarse.**

La recomendación correcta no es únicamente:

What should we do?

También es:

How strongly  
should we believe  
that this is the right move?

Y, sobre todo:

How much  
should we commit  
given that confidence?

La lógica definitiva:

Evidence  
→  
Confidence  
→  
Commitment  
→  
New Evidence  
→  
Updated Confidence  
→  
Updated Commitment

La regla central:

Do not wait for certainty.

Do not pretend certainty.

Commit  
in proportion  
to what you actually know.
