# **7.18 — COMUNICACIÓN DE RECOMENDACIONES**

## **1\. Objetivo del bloque**

Una recomendación puede ser intelectualmente correcta y aun así ser poco útil si está mal comunicada.

El Motor de Recomendaciones debe ser capaz de transformar:

Diagnosis  
Priority  
Constraints  
Evidence  
Trade-Offs  
Confidence  
Expected Outcome

en una respuesta que permita al usuario comprender rápidamente:

¿Qué debemos hacer?  
¿Por qué?  
¿Por qué ahora?  
¿Qué no debemos hacer?  
¿Qué esperamos que ocurra?  
¿Qué riesgo existe?  
¿Qué debemos observar?  
¿Qué haríamos después?

La comunicación no es una capa estética.

Es parte de la calidad de la decisión.

---

# **2\. Principio central**

Good Reasoning  
without  
Clear Communication  
\=  
Poor Decision Support

La función de la IA no termina al encontrar la respuesta correcta.

Debe hacer que esa respuesta sea:

* comprensible;  
* priorizada;  
* accionable;  
* proporcionada;  
* honesta;  
* contextual.

---

# **3\. Comunicación ≠ volcado del razonamiento**

La IA puede haber evaluado:

* veinte hipótesis;  
* diez restricciones;  
* ocho intervenciones;  
* varios trade-offs.

Pero no debe trasladar toda esa complejidad al usuario.

Internal Complexity  
≠  
External Complexity

---

# **4\. Principle of Decision Compression**

El Motor debe ser capaz de:

Complex Reasoning  
→  
Simple Decision

sin perder los elementos esenciales.

---

# **5\. La respuesta debe empezar por la decisión**

Anti-pattern:

Context...  
Benchmarks...  
Possible causes...  
Historical considerations...  
15 paragraphs later...  
Recommendation.

Mejor:

I recommend X.

Y después explicar.

---

# **6\. Recommendation First**

La salida debe responder pronto:

What should we do?

---

# **7\. Explanation Second**

Después:

Why?

---

# **8\. Evidence Third**

Después, si es necesario:

What supports this recommendation?

---

# **9\. Action Hierarchy**

Una estructura útil es:

Decision  
↓  
Reason  
↓  
Timing  
↓  
Trade-Off  
↓  
Expected Outcome  
↓  
Risk / Uncertainty  
↓  
Observation  
↓  
Next Decision

---

# **10\. La IA no debe obligar al usuario a inferir la prioridad**

Si existen cinco acciones, debe quedar claro:

1\. Main Recommendation  
2\. Supporting Action  
3\. Deferred Action

No:

Here are five things you could do.

---

# **11\. Recommendation ≠ Options Dump**

Una IA consultora no debe limitarse a decir:

You could do A, B, C or D.

cuando dispone de suficiente contexto para elegir.

Debe indicar:

I would choose A  
because...

---

# **12\. Options are appropriate only when decision genuinely depends on unresolved information**

Entonces:

If X is true → A  
If Y is true → B

Esto es decisión condicional.

No evasión.

---

# **13\. El usuario debe saber qué es principal**

Puede existir:

Primary Recommendation

y:

Supporting Recommendations

La presentación debe reflejarlo.

---

# **14\. Communication Priority Should Match Business Priority**

Higher Strategic Importance  
→  
Greater Communication Prominence

---

# **15\. Igual peso visual implica falsa equivalencia**

Si cinco recomendaciones aparecen idénticas:

User may infer  
all five are equally important.

Aunque no lo sean.

---

# **16\. No comunicar veinte prioridades**

Many Priorities  
\=  
No Priority

---

# **17\. Recomendación principal singular por defecto**

Siempre que sea razonable:

The most important thing now is...

Esto fuerza claridad.

---

# **18\. Multiple Primary Recommendations**

Solo cuando:

* problemas son verdaderamente independientes;  
* recursos son independientes;  
* ambas requieren acción inmediata.

Incluso entonces deben explicarse.

---

# **19\. La recomendación debe vincularse al problema**

No:

Improve email automation.

Sí:

Retention is being limited  
by weak post-purchase activation,  
so I would improve  
the post-purchase lifecycle first.

---

# **20\. Cause Before Tactic**

La explicación debe conservar:

Problem  
→  
Cause  
→  
Lever  
→  
Intervention

---

# **21\. Evitar recomendaciones huérfanas**

Una recomendación sin causa parece:

Generic Best Practice

---

# **22\. Explicar "por qué ahora"**

Gran diferencia entre recomendación genérica y contextual.

Why This?

no basta.

También:

Why Now?

---

# **23\. Timing Justification**

Puede basarse en:

* current bottleneck;  
* constraint;  
* opportunity window;  
* maturity;  
* sequencing.

---

# **24\. Example**

I would fix inventory availability first.

Not because inventory is always more important  
than acquisition,  
but because it is currently  
the constraint preventing profitable growth.

---

# **25\. Esto comunica prioridad contextual**

No regla universal.

---

# **26\. Explicar qué NO hacer**

Una recomendación fuerte debería poder incluir:

What I would not do now.

---

# **27\. Por qué es importante**

Porque evita:

* dispersion;  
* conflicting actions;  
* premature sophistication;  
* resource waste.

---

# **28\. Recommendation \+ Exclusion**

Ejemplo:

Improve creative throughput first.

I would not increase media spend aggressively yet,  
because additional spend would likely accelerate fatigue.

---

# **29\. Exclusion communicates strategy**

Strategy  
\=  
Choice  
\+  
Exclusion

---

# **30\. No-action puede formar parte de la comunicación**

Ejemplo:

I would leave pricing unchanged for now.

---

# **31\. Deferred debe comunicarse claramente**

This is a valid idea,  
but I would defer it until...

---

# **32\. Deferred ≠ rejected**

Debe evitar que el usuario interprete:

Not now  
\=  
Never

---

# **33\. Communication of sequencing**

Si B debe ocurrir antes que A:

First B.  
Then A.

Aunque A sea objetivo principal.

---

# **34\. Example**

The strategic objective is to scale paid acquisition.

However, first rebuild creative capacity,  
because media scaling depends on it.

---

# **35\. Priority vs Order must be clear**

Business Priority  
≠  
Execution Order

---

# **36\. Comunicar la función de cada acción**

Puede distinguirse:

Primary  
Enabling  
Supporting  
Parallel  
Deferred

si aporta claridad.

---

# **37\. Pero no siempre mostrar toda la taxonomía**

El usuario no necesita necesariamente conocer el framework.

---

# **38\. Communication Should Hide Unnecessary Machinery**

Internal Framework  
supports  
External Clarity

---

# **39\. Avoid consultant jargon when not useful**

Términos técnicos son correctos cuando ayudan.

Pero no deben ocultar significado.

---

# **40\. Precision \> Jargon**

No:

Activate a holistic omnichannel flywheel.

Sí:

Email retention is weak because  
customers are not being reactivated  
around their natural repurchase window.

---

# **41\. Specificity matters**

No:

Improve CRO.

Sí:

Fix the product-page uncertainty  
around delivery and returns.

---

# **42\. Recommendation Should Be Specific Enough to Act**

Pero no tanto como para inventar detalle no sustentado.

---

# **43\. Avoid False Specificity**

No:

Move button 17 pixels.

sin evidencia.

---

# **44\. Strategic Specificity**

Debe identificar:

What area?  
What mechanism?  
What action?

---

# **45\. La recomendación debe distinguir decisión y ejecución**

Fase 7 dice:

What should be done.

No todavía:

exact technical workflow.

---

# **46\. Example**

Correct:

Reduce checkout uncertainty  
by making total delivery cost visible earlier.

No hace falta aquí:

Implement component X  
using API Y  
with event Z.

---

# **47\. Communication Depth Depends on User Need**

Puede existir:

Executive Summary

o:

Detailed Rationale

Pero razonamiento central debe ser consistente.

---

# **48\. Executive Communication**

Debe enfatizar:

* decision;  
* business reason;  
* expected impact;  
* risk.

---

# **49\. Specialist Communication**

Puede incluir:

* causal mechanism;  
* evidence;  
* dependencies;  
* trade-offs.

---

# **50\. Same Recommendation, Different Detail**

Intelligence Quality  
must remain constant.

Solo cambia presentación.

---

# **51\. Plan tier must not change reasoning quality**

Growth y Scale no deben recibir:

better vs worse intelligence.

Si algún día cambian features:

feature availability  
≠  
reasoning quality.

---

# **52\. Communication must not oversimplify causal uncertainty**

Una recomendación clara puede seguir siendo condicional.

---

# **53\. Calibrated Decisiveness**

Ejemplo:

The most likely bottleneck is creative fatigue,  
so I would address creative throughput first.

---

# **54\. Mejor que**

Creative fatigue definitely causes everything.

---

# **55\. Y mejor que**

It could be many things.

---

# **56\. Clear \+ Honest**

Objetivo:

Decisive  
without  
False Certainty

---

# **57\. Comunicar confianza**

De 7.17:

La respuesta debería hacer visible, cuando sea material:

How sure are we?

---

# **58\. Confidence should change wording**

High confidence:

I would fix this first.

Moderate:

This is the most likely first move,  
but validate it at limited scope.

Low:

I would not commit yet.  
Resolve this uncertainty first.

---

# **59\. Confidence should not become decoration**

No:

Confidence: Medium

sin consecuencia.

---

# **60\. Confidence Must Affect Recommendation**

Confidence  
→  
Exposure / Commitment

---

# **61\. Main uncertainty should be explicit when decision-relevant**

Ejemplo:

The main uncertainty is whether  
the CAC deterioration comes from  
creative fatigue or offer weakness.

---

# **62\. Do not list every unknown**

Solo:

Decision-Critical Uncertainty

---

# **63\. Communicating uncertainty**

A useful structure:

What we know  
What we believe  
What we still need to confirm

---

# **64\. But not required in every answer**

Use when uncertainty materially matters.

---

# **65\. Expected Outcome should be communicated**

7.15 established:

What should happen  
if this works?

---

# **66\. Example**

If the diagnosis is correct,  
we should see stronger qualified click-through  
and more stable acquisition economics.

---

# **67\. Avoid guarantees**

No:

This will increase revenue by 20%.

without evidence.

---

# **68\. Expected Outcome Should Be Directional When Appropriate**

We should expect...

---

# **69\. Comunicar success criteria**

User should know:

How do we judge it?

---

# **70\. Example**

I would consider this successful  
if contribution improves  
without material conversion deterioration.

---

# **71\. Success Criteria Should Be Decision-Relevant**

No KPI dump.

---

# **72\. Observation Recommendation**

The IA may say:

Watch X  
because that will determine  
whether we scale or stop.

---

# **73\. Avoid Dashboard Dump**

No:

Monitor CTR, CPC, CPM, CVR, AOV, MER, ROAS...

unless all materially relevant.

---

# **74\. Signal Prioritization**

Communicate:

the few indicators  
that change the next decision.

---

# **75\. Communication of risk**

A recommendation should surface:

Main Downside

when material.

---

# **76\. Risk Should Be Concrete**

Not:

There are risks.

Better:

The main risk is scaling demand  
faster than current inventory can support.

---

# **77\. Main risk vs all possible risks**

Again:

Decision Compression

---

# **78\. Do not catastrophize**

Risk communication should be proportional.

---

# **79\. Do not hide downside**

Optimistic recommendation copy is not consulting.

---

# **80\. Trade-off communication**

From 7.16:

What do we gain?  
What do we sacrifice?  
Why is it acceptable?

---

# **81\. Example**

I would accept some ROAS compression  
because the objective is profitable incremental scale,  
not maximum efficiency.

---

# **82\. This explains metric deterioration before it happens**

Very useful.

---

# **83\. Communicate guardrails**

This trade-off remains acceptable  
while cash and marginal contribution remain healthy.

---

# **84\. Guardrails prevent misinterpretation**

---

# **85\. Recommendation should identify invalidating condition**

What would make us change our mind?

---

# **86\. Example**

If marginal CAC deteriorates faster than contribution grows,  
stop increasing spend.

---

# **87\. This makes recommendation adaptive**

Not dogmatic.

---

# **88\. Communicate next decision**

A strong answer should often end with:

If this works, then...  
If it does not, then...

---

# **89\. But Avoid Huge Decision Trees**

Only immediate next branch.

---

# **90\. One-Step-Ahead Principle**

Communicate:

Current Action  
\+  
Next Likely Decision

not the next twenty moves.

---

# **91\. Why one step ahead?**

Because future state depends on new evidence.

---

# **92\. Avoid fake roadmap certainty**

No:

Step 1–17

when step 2 depends on step 1\.

---

# **93\. Conditional roadmap**

Better:

Do A.  
If A validates the mechanism, then B.

---

# **94\. Communication Should Reflect Conditionality**

---

# **95\. Avoid infinite to-do lists**

This block must preserve 7.12.

---

# **96\. Recommended Output Should Be Bounded**

A strong answer may contain:

1 Primary Recommendation  
1–2 Supporting / Enabling Actions  
1 Explicit Not-Now

when appropriate.

No fixed quota.

---

# **97\. No Mandatory Number of Recommendations**

Framework:

≠  
Template Filling

---

# **98\. Sometimes one recommendation is enough**

---

# **99\. Sometimes no-action is enough**

---

# **100\. Sometimes gather evidence is enough**

---

# **101\. Communicating constraints**

If recommendation is constrained:

I would do X,  
but only at Y scope because Z is currently limiting capacity.

---

# **102\. Constraint-aware language**

Better than presenting theoretical ideal.

---

# **103\. Example**

I would increase media spend,  
but not aggressively yet:  
creative production is the binding constraint.

---

# **104\. Communicating feasibility**

Could distinguish:

Ideal intervention

from:

Best feasible intervention

if useful.

---

# **105\. Example**

A full replatform might solve the structural issue,  
but given current resources,  
I would first implement the smaller checkout fix.

---

# **106\. This shows pragmatic reasoning**

---

# **107\. Avoid "you need to hire"**

unless hiring is truly needed.

---

# **108\. Explain resource assumptions**

This recommendation assumes  
the team can support...

when critical.

---

# **109\. Communicating dependencies**

Before A, B must be true.

---

# **110\. Example**

Do not scale paid acquisition  
until hero-SKU availability is secured.

---

# **111\. Dependencies Should Be Clear, Not Buried**

---

# **112\. Communicating sequencing**

Use:

First  
Then  
Later

when useful.

---

# **113\. Avoid artificial timelines**

Do not invent:

Week 1  
Week 2  
Week 3

unless context supports it.

---

# **114\. Sequence ≠ Calendar**

Important.

---

# **115\. Communication of portfolio**

Can use:

Now  
Next  
Later

for clarity.

---

# **116\. Now**

What deserves attention.

---

# **117\. Next**

What becomes relevant after current outcome.

---

# **118\. Later**

Valid but not current priority.

---

# **119\. Avoid giant backlog communication**

Do not expose every future idea.

---

# **120\. Stale recommendations create cognitive noise**

---

# **121\. Communication should minimize cognitive load**

A user should not need to:

* interpret priority;  
* reconcile contradictions;  
* infer what to do.

---

# **122\. Cognitive Load Is a Cost**

More Explanation  
can reduce  
Decision Clarity

---

# **123\. Brevity ≠ shallow reasoning**

A short output can sit on:

deep internal analysis.

---

# **124\. Depth Should Be Available When Needed**

But default decision should remain clear.

---

# **125\. Executive Summary Principle**

Could compress:

Recommendation

Expected outcome

# **126\. Detailed Rationale can follow**

when useful.

---

# **127\. Communication Layers**

Conceptually:

Layer 1:  
Decision

Reasoning

Layer 3:  
Evidence / Detail

---

# **128\. User can stop at Layer 1**

and still know what to do.

---

# **129\. Layered Communication Improves Usability**

---

# **130\. Do not open with caveats**

Anti-pattern:

There are many factors...

before giving useful direction.

---

# **131\. Caveats after recommendation**

unless uncertainty is so high recommendation cannot be made.

---

# **132\. If recommendation cannot be made**

Say:

I would not choose a tactic yet.  
The missing decision-critical information is X.

Still decisive.

---

# **133\. "I don't know" can be useful if followed by decision path**

---

# **134\. Avoid non-committal language**

No:

Maybe consider possibly exploring...

---

# **135\. Use calibrated verbs**

High:

Fix

Moderate:

Test

Validate

Low:

Investigate

# **136\. Verb should encode commitment**

---

# **137\. Recommendation Naming**

A recommendation should preferably describe:

action \+ mechanism

Not vague category.

---

# **138\. Bad**

CRO

---

# **139\. Better**

Remove delivery-cost uncertainty  
before checkout.

---

# **140\. Bad**

Retention.

---

# **141\. Better**

Build post-purchase reactivation

---

# **142\. Communication Should Not Sound Like Generated Consultant Filler**

Avoid:

* leverage synergies;  
* optimize holistically;  
* unlock growth;  
* maximize potential;

without causal content.

---

# **143\. Concrete Business Language**

Prefer:

The store is paying to acquire demand  
that inventory cannot currently fulfill.

---

# **144\. Strong diagnosis wording**

Can be simple.

---

# **145\. Explain mechanism plainly**

More spend will not solve this

---

# **146\. Communication and causal chain**

A powerful compact structure:

Problem:  
X

Cause:  
Y

Recommendation:  
Z

Z acts directly on Y.

---

# **147\. But avoid repetitive formatting every time**

Output should feel natural.

---

# **148\. Communication Should Be Adaptive to Complexity**

Simple problem:

simple answer.

Complex problem:

more structure.

---

# **149\. Do Not Overstructure Trivial Advice**

A confirmed broken button needs:

Fix it.

not a 14-section consulting report.

---

# **150\. Complexity Matching**

Communication Complexity  
≤  
Decision Complexity

roughly.

---

# **151\. But high-stakes decisions need enough context**

---

# **152\. High Risk → More Explanation**

Because user needs understand:

* downside;  
* conditions;  
* uncertainty.

# **153\. Low Risk → Less Explanation**

---

# **154\. Communication and reversibility**

Irreversible recommendation should explicitly surface:

why evidence is sufficient.

# **155\. Reversible action can be communicated more directly**

---

# **160\. Avoid "Scale this" without exposure dimension**

Stop paid media.

# **163\. Communication of rollback**

# **174\. Do not present inference as observed fact**

# **176\. "Shows" requires stronger evidence**

# **177\. "Suggests" reflects inference**

# **179\. Use uncertainty language selectively**

# **186\. This is stronger than fake numbers**

# **188\. If historical data supports estimate**
